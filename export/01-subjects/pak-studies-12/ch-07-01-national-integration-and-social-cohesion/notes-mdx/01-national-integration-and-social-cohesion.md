## Society

A society is characterised as a collection of individuals who interact with one another in a particular geographic or social setting and who share a shared culture, set of values, customs, and institutions. With numerous social, economic, and cultural facets, societies are intricate systems. They can be grouped according to mutual hobbies, language, race, or religion.

## Community

A community is a more intimate, smaller group within a society. Commonly used characteristics include a sense of belonging, a shared geographic location, and shared hobbies. Communities might be based on the same goals, shared living areas, or cultural or religious customs. Compared to the rest of society, members of a community frequently engage with one another more directly and frequently.

# Pakistan's Diversity and the Role of Minorities

## Diversity of Pakistan

<CaptionedImage src="kg218szwdtaj4x0q8fgd5tywfx8dgapc" alt="" caption="" />
Pakistan Motorways

Pakistan is a country abundant in cultural, religious, ethnic, and regional diversity. Embracing this difference with mutual respect and understanding can transform it into a powerful force that unifies us. It is imperative to acknowledge and appreciate the diverse identities present in Pakistan in order to establish a society that is characterised by harmony and peace.
Let's have a look at different kinds of diversity we have in Pakistan:

## Ethnic Diversity

Pakistan is home to a rich tapestry of ethnic groups, including Punjabis, Sindhis, Pashtuns, Baloch, Saraiki, Hindku, Kashmiri, Gilgiti, Balti, and other such ethnic groups. Each group brings its own unique traditions, customs, and social structures, contributing to the vibrant mosaic of Pakistani society. This ethnic diversity is a source of cultural richness and plays a vital role in the nation's identity and social fabric.

## Cultural Diversity

The cultural diversity of Pakistan is a reflection of its varied ethnic groups and historical influences. From the traditional dresses, music and dance forms like Bhangra and Khattak to the diverse culinary traditions found in different regions, Pakistan's cultural landscape is both dynamic and colorful. Festivals such as the Horse \& Cattle Show, Sibi Mela, Shindoor Festival and other such events are celebrated with enthusiasm, showcasing the country's deep-rooted cultural heritage.

## Religious Diversity

While Islam is the predominant religion in Pakistan, the country also has significant populations of Christians, Hindus, Sikhs, and other religious communities. This religious diversity is protected under the Constitution of Pakistan, which guarantees freedom of religion. The presence of various religious communities contributes to promoting a culture of coexistence and mutual respect.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2a1k5as7qppk0vqy349vk4f58dhf5w" alt="" caption="" />

## Linguistic Diversity

Pakistan's linguistic diversity is extensive, with several major languages spoken across the country, including Urdu, Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Brahvi, Saraiki, Hindku, Kashmiri, Pahari, Shina, Broshiski, Persian and Khwar, among others. Urdu serves as the national language and a symbol of unity, while each region proudly uses its own language for everyday communication and cultural expression. This multilingualism enhances the cultural depth and connectivity of the Pakistani people.

## Geographical Diversity

Geographically, Pakistan boasts a diverse landscape that includes the towering peaks of the Himalayas and Karakoram ranges, the fertile plains of Punjab and Sindh, the arid deserts of Thar and Cholistan, and the scenic coastal areas along the Arabian Sea in Balochistan. This geographical diversity supports a wide range of ecosystems and climates, fostering a rich biodiversity and providing numerous opportunities for agriculture, tourism, and economic development.
This diversity must be welcomed and celebrated if Pakistan is to develop a strong and comprehensive national identity. A concerted effort to promote crosscultural dialogue, understanding, and respect for other identities contributes to social harmony and national unity.

## Mutual Respect for a Peaceful Society

Understanding and respect for one another are fundamental components of a strong and cohesive community. Respect for one another and an effort to understand one another among individuals and groups foster a positive atmosphere that increases collaboration, reduces conflict, and strengthens social cohesion.
Mutual respect is essential for promoting social harmony, building trust and cooperation among diverse groups, reducing conflicts, strengthening national unity, enhancing socio-economic development, fostering peaceful coexistence, and breaking stereotypes and prejudices. It is crucial to incorporate diversity in education, public awareness campaigns, community engagement, and supportive policies and leadership.
Education should teach students about the importance of diversity, respect, and understanding from a young age, promoting empathy and inclusivity. A rich curriculum should be developed that gives liberty to think and analyse. Public awareness campaigns can raise awareness about the benefits of mutual respect and understanding, while community engagement can be fostered
through interfaith and intercultural dialogues. Community events and celebrations can provide opportunities for people to learn about and appreciate each other's traditions.
Government policies should promote inclusion and protect the rights of all citizens, ensuring equal opportunities and fair treatment for everyone. Leaders at all levels should model mutual respect and understanding, inspiring their communities to adopt similar attitudes.
By fostering a culture of mutual respect and understanding, Pakistan can foster a more inclusive and peaceful society.

## Role of Minorities in Creation and Development of Pakistan

As mentioned earlier, Pakistan has a rich religious diversity and the Constitution of Pakistan gives rights to all its citizens irrespective of their religious background. The people belonging to religions other than Islam also played a significant role earlier in the creation and then in the development of Pakistan.
Let's have a look at their contributions to economy, education, healthcare, public service, governance, arts, culture and sports.

## Economic Contributions

Minorities in Pakistan have made significant contributions to the economy through various industries, including business, agriculture, and manufacturing. Notable minority communities, such as the Parsis, Christians, and Hindus, have been instrumental in establishing and growing businesses that provide employment and drive economic growth.

## Education and Healthcare

Minority communities have played a crucial role in the development of Pakistan's education and healthcare sectors. Many schools, colleges, and hospitals established by Christian missionaries and other minority groups have been at the forefront of providing quality education and healthcare services to people of all backgrounds.

## Public Service and Governance

Individuals from minority communities have held important positions in public service and governance, contributing to the administration and development of the state. Their participation in politics, civil service, and the judiciary has enriched the governance framework with diverse perspectives and expertise.

## Quaid-e-Azam's Vision for Religious Freedom

Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah envisioned a state where religious freedom and equality were paramount.

Quaid e Azam believed that minorities played a crucial role in the development of Pakistan. He envisioned a nation where every citizen, irrespective of their religious or ethnic background, would have equal opportunities to contribute to the socio-economic and cultural growth of the country. Quaid's vision was for a pluralistic society where minorities could participate fully in public life, government, and the economy. He believed that by ensuring equal rights and protections for all, Pakistan could harness the talents and contributions of its diverse population, leading to a stronger, more cohesive nation.
<CaptionedImage src="kg2bm3pwn8qbpdgxtdxj8nbrhx8dg815" alt="" caption="" />

During a press conference in July 1947, Quaid-e-Azam said while replying to a question related to minority rights:
> "There will be no interference of any kind with their freedom of worship. They will have their protection with regard to their religion, faith, their life, their property and their culture. They will be in all respects treated as citizens of Pakistan without any distinction of caste, colour, religion or creed. They will have all their rights and privileges and also the obligations of citizenship. Therefore, the minorities have their responsibilities also and they will play their part in the affairs of the state. As long as the minorities are loyal to the state and owe true allegiance to it and as long as I have any power, they need have no apprehension of any kind."

In his historic speech on August 11, 1947, Jinnah articulated his vision for Pakistan, emphasizing that all citizens, regardless of their religious beliefs, would be free to practice their faith without fear or discrimination. He said:
> "You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other place of worship in this State of Pakistan. You may belong to any religion or caste or creed-that has nothing to do with the business of the State."

This commitment to religious freedom was intended to foster an inclusive society where diversity was respected and all citizens could contribute to the nation's development.

Quaid-e-Azam's vision for Pakistan included the establishment of a culture of peace and harmony. He understood that respect for religious freedom and the inclusion of minorities were essential for social stability and national unity. By promoting tolerance and understanding among different religious and ethnic groups, Quaid e Azam aimed to create a peaceful society where conflicts were minimized, and cooperation was maximized. This vision of a peaceful and inclusive Pakistan was rooted in the principles of justice, equality, and human dignity, which Jinnah believed were essential for the nation's prosperity and cohesion.

# Welfare State, Solidarity and National Unity

## Welfare State

A welfare state is a governmental system that prioritizes the well-being and prosperity of its citizens by ensuring equal opportunities, equitable resource distribution, and social justice. In such a state, the government actively works to reduce inequalities and provide a safety net for all, particularly addressing issues related to gender, religion, and social classes.
In this state, equality means that all citizens have equal rights and opportunities regardless of their gender, religion, or social class. This includes equal access to education, healthcare, employment, and political participation. The state enacts laws and policies that prohibit discrimination and promote equal treatment for everyone.

In a welfare state equity is also ensured that goes beyond equality by recognizing that different individuals and groups may have different needs and starting points. A welfare state ensures that resources are distributed in a way that addresses these disparities. This might involve targeted programs and support for marginalized or disadvantaged groups, such as women, religious minorities, and lower social classes, to level the playing field and provide everyone with fair opportunities to succeed.
Justice is another quality of a welfare state that involves creating a legal and social environment where everyone is treated fairly and with respect. This includes the protection of human rights, access to legal recourse, and the assurance that societal institutions are transparent and accountable. The state works to eliminate systemic biases and prejudices, ensuring that all citizens can live with dignity and security.

## Who Establishes a Welfare State?

Establishing a welfare state does not just rest on the shoulders of a government. This task is equally shared by both the state and society. Establishing and functioning a welfare state necessitates collaboration and interaction among the government, society, and state. Each person makes a distinct and valuable contribution to creating and maintaining a system that prioritizes the well-being of its residents.

The state formulates broad policies and constitutional provisions to ensure a framework of equality, equity, and justice. In contrast, the government is responsible for the day-to-day implementation of these policies through legislation and public services. Society plays a crucial role in advocating for these rights and holding the state and government accountable.
For instance, while the state ensures constitutional rights for all citizens, the government enacts specific laws like the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act to address gender equality practically. Civil society organizations advocate these rights and take necessary steps when these rights are violated.

## Role of the State

The state in Pakistan plays a crucial role in framing the overarching policies and constitutional framework necessary for establishing a welfare state that ensures equality, equity, and justice. The state sets the direction for social and economic development, enshrining fundamental rights in the Constitution of Pakistan.

Constitutional guarantees like Article 25 ensure equality of all citizens before the law and prohibit discrimination based on sex alone. Similarly, Articles 21 and 36 protect the rights of minorities, ensuring their inclusion and safeguarding their cultural, religious, and

## Role of Society

Society in Pakistan, through its various civil society organizations, community groups, and informal networks, plays a pivotal role in advocating for and implementing the principles of a welfare state at the grassroots level. Societal attitudes and collective actions can significantly influence the realization of equality, equity, and justice.
There are many nongovernment organizations (NGOs) and civil society platforms that work on gender equality and women's empowerment, advocating for women's rights and providing support services for victims of gender-based violence. Similarly, platforms like the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP) focus on promoting human rights and addressing issues related to social justice, including the rights of marginalized communities.
In addition, there are many community initiatives such as Edhi Foundation that provide extensive welfare services, including healthcare, orphanages, and emergency relief, embodying the spirit of social justice and equity.

## Role of the Government

The government of Pakistan is responsible for implementing state policies, enacting laws, and providing public services that ensure equality, equity, and justice. Government actions directly affect the daily lives of citizens and their access to essential services.
Certain legislative measures have been taken by the government of Pakistan such as "the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010", which was enacted to provide a safe working environment for women and ensure their protection against harassment. ## Unification and Solidarity of a Nation

Protecting the country against internal and foreign threats to its security depends on the national identity being consolidated by national integration. There is a subtle difference between centripetal and centrifugal forces; the former creates an adverse environment with certain properties. This setting so affects several languages, cultures, races, and religions, all of which having the possibility to either contribute to national unity or fracture.

## Elements Contributing to Solidarity

There are several elements that contribute to national unity and cohesion. Understanding these elements is crucial for fostering a strong and united Pakistan.

## National Identity and Patriotism

Promoting a sense of shared identity and pride in being Pakistani helps unite diverse groups. Celebrating national symbols, history, and achievements fosters a collective national , consciousness. Incorporating patriotism into the education system through history lessons, national heroes, and the significance of independence can instill a sense of unity and pride.

## Inclusive Governance and Political Stability

Strong, transparent, and inclusive democratic institutions that represent all segments of society contribute to national unity by ensuring that everyone has a voice. Efficient and corruption-free governance that delivers public services equitably helps build trust in the state and promotes solidarity. In Pakistan, the upper house, the Senate is the symbol of equal representation of federating units.

## Cultural and Religious Harmony

Promoting dialogue and understanding among different religious communities helps build respect and reduce tensions. Encouraging the celebration of cultural diversity through festivals, arts, and cultural exchanges enhances mutual respect and understanding. Moreover, helping the youth to discover the diversity of the country by exposing them to different cultures may bring fruitful results.

## Social and Economic Equity

Ensuring that all regions and communities have access to economic opportunities and development projects reduces disparities and fosters a sense of belonging. Implementing social welfare programs that address the needs of the poor and marginalized groups helps create a more cohesive society. In this regard, it is crucial that more focus should be given to equity than equality.

## Awareness through Education

Educating citizens about their rights, responsibilities, and the importance of unity in diversity fosters informed and engaged citizenship. Responsible media and positive communication campaigns can promote unity by highlighting common goals and values.

## Challenges Impeding Unification and Solidarity

The processes related to the unification and solidarity of the nation are often challenged by various impediments. Some of these challenges are as under:

## Ethnic and Sectarian Divisions

Pakistan's diverse ethnic composition can sometimes lead to tensions and conflicts, particularly when certain groups feel marginalized or discriminated against. Religious and sectarian differences, if not managed carefully, can lead to violence and division, undermining national solidarity. The faith leaders from different sects and media must play a responsible role in meeting this challenge successfully.

## Political Instability and Corruption

Political instability, power struggles, and lack of consensus among political leaders can weaken national unity. This gets worse when the political leaders take extreme position that causes further polarization. Corruption erodes trust in government institutions and creates a sense of injustice, contributing to social fragmentation. Another adverse effect of the situation is when the government-allotted funds for marginalized communities do not reach them because of corruption.

## Economic Inequality

Unequal economic development across different regions can lead to feelings of neglect and resentment, undermining national cohesion. High levels of poverty and unemployment can lead to social unrest and hinder efforts to build a unified nation. It is important to offer special initiatives for such regions such as Aghaz e Haqooq e Balochistan and special initiatives for erstwhile FATA.

Extended external threats or disputes with neighbouring countries have the ability to divert attention and resources, therefore fostering an attitude of external adversary rather than internal cohesiveness. Pakistan borders India, hence previous conflicts and boundary disputes there, might sour diplomatic ties.
