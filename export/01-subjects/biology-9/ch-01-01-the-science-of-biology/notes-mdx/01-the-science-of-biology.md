# 1.1 Introduction to Biology

Science is a process of collecting information about the world around us. When you look at a plant, you observe its leaves and flowers and begin to wonder: *why are the leaves green? Why are the flowers of different colours?* Asking such questions is the first step in doing science. Making observations, asking questions, and trying to find their answers is what science is all about. The study of science helps us answer the *how*, *what*, *where*, and *why* of our surroundings.

## Definition of Biology

The word *biology* is made of two Greek words:

- **bios**, meaning life
- **logos**, meaning thought, reasoning, and study

Biology is the study of living organisms. It helps us explain how living things relate to one another and to their surroundings.

## Quranic Perspective on the Origin of Life

What science is discovering today, the Holy Quran hinted at several hundred years ago. The Holy Quran is a book for all times to come, giving us spiritual, moral, and practical knowledge. Several of its verses describe the origin of life; a few are quoted here.

### Origin of Life in Water

<Ayah arabic="وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ حَيٍّ" reference="Surah Al-Anbiya (21:30)">We made every living thing from water</Ayah>

Living things consist of 60 to 90 percent water. All living things have come out of water and therefore share a common origin.

### Creation of Man

<Ayah arabic="خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِن صَلْصَالٍ كَالْفَخَّارِ" reference="Surah Ar-Rahman (55:14)">He made man from clay like the potter</Ayah>

The creation of man consisted of two steps. The first was creation from water; the second was mixing clay with water to create man. The same can be said for all animals, as man shares all the characteristics of life with other animals.

### Development

<Ayah arabic="ثُمَّ خَلَقْنَا النُّطْفَةَ عَلَقَةً فَخَلَقْنَا الْعَلَقَةَ مُضْغَةً فَخَلَقْنَا الْمُضْغَةَ عِظَامًا فَكَسَوْنَا الْعِظَامَ لَحْمًا" reference="Surah Al-Mu'minoon (23:14)">Then fashioned we the drop a clot, then fashioned we the clot a little lump, then fashioned we the little lump bones, then clotted the bones with flesh</Ayah>

The sequence of these developmental stages is described in the Quran many times.

## Contributions of Muslim Scientists

Muslim scientists have made great contributions to the field of biology. The work of **Jabir Bin Hayyan**, **Abdul Malik Asmai**, and **Bu Ali Sina** contributed greatly to the present-day knowledge of plants and animals.

## Main Divisions of Biology

Biology has three main divisions:

- **Botany**, the study of plants.
- **Zoology**, the study of animals.
- **Microbiology**, the study of microorganisms, e.g. viruses and bacteria.

# 1.2 Major Fields of Biology

Biology is divided into a number of sub-fields, making its study more convenient.

## Sub-fields and Their Areas of Study

| Field | What it studies |
| :--- | :--- |
| Morphology | Size, shape, and structure of organisms |
| Anatomy | Internal structure of organisms |
| Physiology | Functions of organs |
| Histology | Tissues under the microscope |
| Cytology | Structure and functions of cells |
| Genetics | Genes and heredity |
| Molecular biology | Biology at the molecular level |
| Embryology | Development from a fertilized egg |
| Paleontology | History of life through fossils |
| Taxonomy | Classification and naming of organisms |
| Ecology | Relationships between organisms and their environment |
| Marine biology | Organisms that live in the sea |
| Pathology | Diseases and their diagnosis |
| Immunology | Immunity |
| Pharmacology | Drugs |

### Morphology

Morphology is the study of the size, shape, and structure of animals, plants, and microorganisms. For example, the morphology of a flowering plant includes its roots, stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits. Dental structure in humans is an example of human morphology.

### Anatomy

Anatomy is the study of the internal structure of organisms. It is also called *internal morphology*. Examples include body parts such as muscles, the heart, brain, and kidneys.

### Physiology

Physiology is the study of the functions of various organs in organisms. Examples include digestion, respiration, excretion, and photosynthesis.

### Histology

Histology is the microscopic study of tissues in organisms. Epithelial tissue, which forms a continuous layer covering the body surface, is an example.

<CaptionedImage src="kg260fja4yjf59agdgymf3fvh58ccmd0" alt="Diagram of epithelial tissue showing closely packed cells forming a continuous layer." caption="Figure 1.2. Epithelial tissue." />

### Cytology

Cytology is the study of the structure and functions of cells. It is also called *cell biology*. The study of plant and animal cells is an example.

### Genetics

Genetics is the study of genes and heredity in organisms. For example, plants with red flowers produce red flowers, and white cats produce white kittens.

### Molecular Biology

Molecular biology is the study of biology at the molecular level.

### Embryology

Embryology is the study of the development of an organism from a fertilized egg.

### Paleontology

Paleontology is the study of the history of life on Earth based on fossils. Fossils are the remains of living things preserved by natural processes. Their study helps us understand past life and the process of evolution.


<CaptionedImage src="kg2799c02nb9f4y7anrdskeydx8ccsm6" alt="Figure 1.3. Fossils preserved in rock." caption="Figure 1.3. Fossils preserved in rock." />


### Taxonomy

Taxonomy is the classification and naming of organisms. For example, humans are mammals and their scientific name is *Homo sapiens*.

### Ecology

Ecology is the study of the interrelationship of organisms and their environment. It is also known as *environmental biology*. Examples include the study of the ecology of ponds, lakes, forests, and deserts.

### Marine Biology

Marine biology is the study of organisms that live in the sea. Examples include fish, whales, dolphins, porpoises, sponges, crustaceans, and molluscs.

### Pathology

Pathology is the branch of science that deals with the study and diagnosis of diseases.

### Immunology

Immunity is the ability of the body to protect itself from foreign substances and cells, including infectious microbes. Immunology is the study of immunity. Immunization is the process by which a person becomes protected against a disease through vaccination. The text gives polio, corona, and dengue as examples of diseases for which vaccines are used.

### Pharmacology

Pharmacology is the science that deals with the study of drugs. A drug is a chemical substance. Aspirin is a drug often used to treat pain, fever, and inflammation. Other examples include morphine, insulin, and penicillin.

The word *pharmacology* is derived from the Greek word *pharmakon*, meaning "drug" or "poison", together with *logia*, meaning "study of" or "knowledge of".

### Computational Biology

The text notes that computational biology has helped sequence the human genome, create accurate models of the human brain, map the three-dimensional structure of genomes, and model biological systems.

# 1.3 Relationship of Biology with Other Sciences

## Biology and Other Sciences

Biology is integrated with other disciplines of science. Animals move, walk, and run according to principles of physics, and the working principle of a lever is similar to that of human limbs.

The behaviour and structure of atoms and molecules help explain the behaviour of living cells. Their physical structure determines their chemical properties and the roles they play in cells. Therefore, a basic knowledge of chemistry is necessary to understand biology.

Biology is not an isolated science. It is associated with several other branches of science.

<CaptionedImage src="kg24cgnh8ath7emsbhcnjeyfyx8ccpw7" alt="Diagram placing Biology at the centre and showing its links with Physics, Chemistry, Computing, Geography, Economics, and Statistics." caption="Figure 1.3. Relationship of biology with other sciences." />
## Fields Connecting Biology with Other Sciences

| Field | What it studies |
| :--- | :--- |
| Biophysics | Biological phenomena according to the principles and laws of physics, such as movement of muscles and bones. |
| Biochemistry | Chemical constituents and reactions in living organisms, including digestion, respiration, and photosynthesis. |
| Biostatistics | Collection and analysis of biological data from observations and experiments according to statistical rules. It is also called biometry. |
| Computational biology | Data analysis, mathematical modelling, and computational simulations used to understand biological systems, such as locating DNA fragments on chromosomes. |
| Biogeography | Distribution of plants and animals in different geographical regions of the world. |
| Biotechnology | Techniques used to manipulate living organisms for the benefit of humankind. |
| Bio-economics | Biology from an economic point of view, for example production and export value of wheat, fish, and rice. |

# 1.5 Science Is a Collaborative Field

## Science Through Collaboration

Scientists around the world work together to share ideas and make progress in research. Some study similar problems, while others bring different knowledge that can help find solutions.

When researchers from different fields work together to create scientific knowledge, this is called *interdisciplinary research collaboration*. It helps researchers share knowledge, critique ideas, solve problems, and make new discoveries.

Examples include:

- **Cognitive science** combines neurology, psychology, anthropology, linguistics, environmental science, engineering, pharmacology, and statistics.
- **Womens Studies** combines gender, history, literature, and biology.
- **Public health** combines medicine, sociology, and psychology.
- **Bioinformatics** combines biology and information technology to understand complex biological data.

Emerging biology careers include bioinformaticians, biomedical engineers, astrobiologists, and cryobiologists. Collaboration during the COVID era showed how working across borders, cultures, and fields benefits science and society. The International Space Station is another example, involving space agencies from Europe, the USA, Russia, and Japan.

# 1.6 Biological Method

Scientists, including biologists, use the scientific method to solve biological problems. Curiosity, exploration, and asking questions are natural skills that help scientists investigate problems.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2a626yz39eax1aq47zxb3xfs8e65p3" alt="The steps of the scientific method." caption="The steps of the scientific method." />

The biological method uses these steps:

1. Recognition of a biological problem
2. Observation and identification
3. Formulation of a hypothesis
4. Drawing deductions
5. Experimentation
6. Summarization of results
## Recognition of the Biological Problem

A biological problem is a question related to living organisms. It may be asked by someone or arise in the mind of a researcher.

## Observations

Observations are an important step in solving a biological problem. They are made using the five senses: vision, hearing, smell, taste, and touch.

- **Qualitative observations** are based on a quality or characteristic.
- **Quantitative observations** are based on measurable values. Because they can be expressed in numbers, they are more accurate and invariable.

## Formulation of Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a tentative statement that may answer the biological problem under study. It explains observations and might be true.

A hypothesis should be:

- A general statement
- A tentative idea
- Consistent with available observations
- Testable and potentially falsifiable

## Deductions

Deductions are the logical consequences of a hypothesis. To draw deductions, the hypothesis is taken as true. Deductions use if-then logic.

## Experimentation

Experimentation tests the deductions drawn from a hypothesis. By carrying out rigorous experiments, a biologist learns which hypothesis is correct.

## Summarization of Results

The biologist gathers quantitative data from experiments, arranges the data, and draws results.

# 1.7 Hypothesis, Theory and Law

## Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a tentative answer to a question. It is based on past experience and available data. A scientific hypothesis makes predictions that can be tested through further observations or experiments.

In deduction-based science, reasoning uses if-then logic: if a hypothesis is correct, then particular experimental or observational outcomes should be expected. The hypothesis is tested by performing the experiment and checking whether the results match those predictions.

## Theory, Data Analysis, and Law

A scientific theory is broader in scope than a hypothesis and is generally supported by more evidence. Scientists may modify or reject even a widely accepted theory when new research methods produce results that do not fit it.

A theory that has been verified and appears to have wide application may become a biological law, for example Mendel law of inheritance.

Data is a collection of facts or information. Scientists organize data using tables and graphs. Predicting on the basis of data is called analysis, which can use ratio and proportion.

# 1.8 Malaria an Example of Biological Method of Study

# 1.8 Malaria: An Example of the Biological Method

Malaria has killed more people than any other disease. It is a clear example of a biological problem and shows how such problems are solved using the biological method.

## Malaria

**Symptoms of malaria:**

- The patient feels very chilled and cold.
- Body temperature rises above the normal value of $98.6^{\circ}\text{F}$.
- The patient suffers from headache and nausea.
- After some time the person begins to sweat and then feels better.
- This series of events repeats every 24, 48, or 72 hours, depending on the species of *Plasmodium*.

<CaptionedImage src="kg25rqwy4zbqh2psz3he3670ns8cdjc9" alt="Flowchart of the scientific (biological) method of study" caption="Fig. 1.6 Scientific method of study." />
## Cause of Malaria

By applying the steps of the biological method, it was proved that malaria is caused by *Plasmodium*.

- **Recognition of the problem:** Malaria had been a problem since ancient times, but its cause was unknown.
- **Observations:** In the 19th century, several causes were suggested. Four major observations stood out:
  - Malaria and marshy areas are related in some way.
  - Quinine is an effective drug for treating malaria.
  - Drinking the water of marshes does not cause malaria.
  - *Plasmodium* is seen in the blood of malarial patients.
- **Hypothesis:** Based on these observations, the French physician Laveran formulated a hypothesis in 1882: *"Plasmodium is the cause of malaria."*
- **Deduction:** *"If Plasmodium is the cause of malaria, then all persons ill with malaria should have Plasmodium in their blood."*
- **Experiment:** The blood of 100 malarial patients was examined under a microscope. As a control group, the blood of 100 healthy persons was also examined.
- **Results:** Almost all malarial patients had *Plasmodium* in their blood. Only 7 of the 100 healthy persons had it, and in those 7 it was in the *incubation period* (the time between the entry of the parasite and the appearance of symptoms); after a few days they too became ill with malaria.
- **Reporting the results:** The results were announced worldwide and helped to control malaria.

## Spread of Malaria

The biological method also helped establish that mosquitoes spread malaria.

- **Recognition of the problem:** After it was confirmed that *Plasmodium* causes malaria, the next problem was to find how it enters human blood. Since drinking marsh water does not cause malaria, *Plasmodium* is not present in the water itself but must be carried by something that visits the marshes.
- **Observations:** In 1883, the American scientist A. F. A. King listed 20 observations. Important ones were:
  - People who slept outdoors were more likely to get malaria than those who slept indoors.
  - People who slept under fine nets were less likely to get malaria.
  - People who slept near a smoky fire usually did not get malaria.
- **Hypothesis:** King suggested: *"Mosquitoes transmit Plasmodium and are involved in the spread of malaria."*
- **Deductions:** *Plasmodium* should be present in the mosquito, and a mosquito can get *Plasmodium* by biting a malarial patient.
- **Experiments:** Ronald Ross, a British army physician in India, allowed a female *Anopheles* mosquito to bite a malarial patient and found *Plasmodium* multiplying in the mosquito's stomach. Using sparrows, he showed that *Culex* mosquitoes could carry malaria from an infected bird to a healthy one. Finally, an Italian biologist confirmed transmission in humans.
- **Results:** These experiments confirmed that mosquitoes transmit *Plasmodium* and spread malaria.

When a female mosquito pierces the skin, it injects saliva that prevents the blood from clotting before it draws blood. A **vector** is any organism that carries a parasite and transfers it from one organism to another.
## Dengue Fever

Dengue fever is caused by the *Dengue virus* and is transmitted by the mosquito *Aedes aegypti*, which has zebra-like white and black stripes on its body.

- **Symptoms:** high-grade fever, bleeding from the nose, blood in the urine, and an enlarged liver.
- **Treatment:** there is no specific antiviral drug; a second attack can be more serious and dangerous.
- **Prevention:** the best prevention is personal protection from mosquito bites and measures to prevent mosquito breeding.
