# 5.1 Algebraic Expressions

## Algebraic Expressions

A statement in which variables or constants or both are connected by arithmetic operations (i.e.,,$+- \times, \div$ ) is called an algebraic expression.

For example,
$\frac{-5 x^{2}+4}{4}, 3(\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b})-4,0,-5$
$\mathrm{z}-\sqrt{2} \mathrm{t}, \frac{1}{x}, \frac{x}{y+1} \sqrt{b^{2}-4 a c}$ etc.

- Numbers
- Signs of operations $(+,-, \times, \div)$

# 5.2 Kinds of Algebraic Expressions

## Kinds of Algebraic Expressions

Algebraic expressions are of three kinds.

- Polynomial Expressions
- Rational Expressions
- Irrational Expressions


## (a) Polynomial Expressions (Polynomials)

Polynomials are algebraic expressions consisting of one or more terms in which exponents of the variables involved are non-negative (either zero or positive) integers.
For example:
$0,-2, \frac{3}{4} x+\frac{3}{4} y^{2} z,-\sqrt{\frac{3}{9}} y^{3}, \sqrt{2} x^{4}-\pi x^{2}-\sqrt{10}$ etc.
The expressions $x^{-3}, y^{2}+\frac{1}{y^{2}}, \sqrt[3]{y^{4}}, 2 y^{\frac{1}{2}}$ are not polynomials because their exponents are not non negative integers (whole numbers).

## (a) Types of Polynomials w.r.t. Terms

- A polynomial having one term is called a monomial. e.g., $3, x, 0$ etc.
- A polynomial having two terms is called a binomial.
e.g. $\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b}, 2 x+y, 7 \mathrm{ab}-\frac{1}{3}, x^{\frac{1}{3}}+2 \frac{1}{4}$ etc.
- A polynomial having three terms is called a trinomial.
e.g. $x^{2}+2 x y+y^{2}, \frac{1}{8} x^{3}+\frac{3}{4} x^{2}-9$ etc.
All other polynomials having more than three non- zero terms are called polynomials of four terms, five terms, six terms and so on.


## Kev Fact:

When a polynomial is written in descending order, then the first coefficient is called the leading coefficient. e.g., in $\mathrm{p}(x)=3 x^{4}-2 x^{2}+1$, the leading coefficient is 3 . Here $\mathrm{p}(x)$ means that the variable in the polynomial is ' $x$ '.

## (b) Types of Polynomials w.r.t. Degree

- Zero polynomial or no degree polynomial:
' 0 ' is called a polynomial of no degree. Also, $0 x^{3}+0 x$ is a no degree polynomial, because coefficients are always zero in zero polynomial.


## - Constant polynomial:

A polynomial having degree zero is called a constant polynomial. e.g. $2,-5, \frac{1}{2}, \sqrt{5}$ are all constant polynomials.

## - Linear polynomial:

A polynomial having degree one is called a linear polynomial. e.g., $x, 2 x-y,-7 x y^{0}$ etc.

- Quadratic polynomial: A polynomial having degree two is called a quadratic polynomial. e.g., $2 x^{2}+7, a x+2 x y+3,-\frac{3}{4} x y z^{0}$ etc.


## - Cubic polynomial:

A polynomial having degree three is called a cubic polynomial. e.g. $9 x^{3}-7 x+5$, $9 x z y, 3 x^{2} y-\frac{3}{4} z$ etc.
All other polynomials have no specific name w.r.t. degree but simply, we call them polynomials of degree four, degree five and so on.

## (c) Types of Polynomials w.r.t. Coefficients

Polynomials can also be classified according to the coefficients of the variables involved in each term of them. e.g., $-3 x^{2}+4 x-1$ is a polynomial with integers co-efficients because $-3,4,-1 \in Z$.

Similarly, $\frac{1}{2} y^{3}-\pi x^{2} y+\frac{22}{7}$ is a polynomial with real numbers because $\frac{1}{2},-\pi, \frac{22}{7} \in R$.
Just like this we can also check the coefficients in any polynomial and classify it accordingly.

## (d) Types of Polynomials w.r.t. Variables

Classification of polynomials is also possible with respect to the variables involved in them. e.g., $x^{2} y-7$ is a polynomial in two variables $x, y$. We can express it as $\mathrm{P}(x, y)=x^{2} y-7$.
Similarly, $6 x^{2} y z+12 x y^{6} z^{6}+9 z^{4}$ is a polynomial in three variables $x, y, z$. We may write it as $\mathrm{Q}(x, y, z)=6 x^{2} y z+12 x y^{6} z^{6}+9 z^{4}$.

## Recall

The highest exponent of the variable involved in a polynomial is called its degree. If more than one variable are being multiplied in terms of a polynomial. Then the degree of that polynomial is the sum of exponent of all the variables present in a term.

## (b) Rational Expression

An algebraic expression of the form $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$,where $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ are polynomials and $\mathrm{Q}(\mathrm{x}) \neq 0$
(i.e. it is not a zero polynomial) is called a rational expression.

For example: $1 \frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{4 x^{2}}, \frac{2 x-1}{x^{2}+3}, \frac{2 x+4}{x^{2}+5 x+6}, \frac{x^{2}+x}{x^{3}+x}, 5$ etc.

## Reduction / Simplification of Rational Expressions

In algebraic rational expressions, we can multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by a same non-zero expression.

## Memory Plus:

Rational expression are the expression in which exponent of variable are integers.
e.g. $\quad \frac{x^{2}+x}{x^{3}+x}=\frac{\left(x^{2}+x\right) \div x}{\left(x^{3}+x\right) \div x}=\frac{x+1}{x^{2}+1}$
Hence, $\frac{x+1}{x^{2}+1}$ is the lowest or reduced form of $\frac{x^{2}+x}{x^{3}+x}$.
Reduction of a rational expression is done on the same rules as in reduction of a rational number. To reduce a rational expression to its lowest form, we have to

- Factorize the numerator and denominator completely.
- Divide the numerator and denominator by their common factors.
- Rewrite the remaining terms as a fraction, computing all products. This will be the reduced form.

Example: Reduce each rational expression to its lowest form.
(a) $\frac{x^{2} y^{3}}{x^{5} y^{3}}$
(b) $\frac{b-a}{a^{3}-b^{3}}$

Solution: (i) By using laws of exponents, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^{2} y^{3}}{x^{5} y^{3}} & =\frac{y^{3-3}}{x^{5-2}} \\
& =\frac{y^{0}}{x^{3}}=\frac{1}{x^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii) Since, $\left(a^{3}-b^{3}\right)=(a-b) \cdot\left(a^{2}+a b+b^{2}\right)$

$$
\text { So, } \begin{aligned}
& \frac{b-a}{a^{3}-b^{3}}=\frac{(b-a)}{(a-b)\left(a^{2}+a b+b^{2}\right)} \\
= & \frac{-(a-b)^{4}}{(a-b)\left(a^{2}+a b+b^{2}\right)} \\
= & \frac{-1}{a^{2}+a b+b^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## (c) Irrational Expression

An algebraic expression which cannot be expressed in the form $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$, where $P(\mathrm{x})$ and $Q(\mathrm{x})$ are polynomials but $Q$ is not a zero polynomial, is called an irrational expression.
For example: $\frac{x}{\sqrt{y}}, x^{\frac{3}{2}} y-7, \sqrt{x}-\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{y}}, \sqrt[5]{x^{2}+y^{2}}, 24 x^{\frac{3}{2}} y^{-2}+\frac{9}{y^{2}}-7$ etc.

# 5.3 Evaluation of Algebraic Expressions

## Evaluation of Algebraic Expressions

The process of replacing the variables by numbers in an algebraic expression is called 'substitution'. The process of getting a numerical answer by substituting numbers for each variable is called 'evaluation'.

Example: Find the value of $\frac{3}{4} x^{2}-\frac{2}{3} x+1$ at $x=\frac{-1}{2}$.
Solution: $\quad \frac{3}{4} x^{2}-\frac{2}{3} x+1$
By substituting the value of $x$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{-1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{-1}{2}\right)+1 \\
& =\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)+1
\end{aligned}
$$

Irrational expressions are those expression in which exponent of variable are non-integers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{3}{16}+\frac{1}{3}+1 \\
& =\frac{9+16+48}{48}=\frac{73}{48}=1 \frac{25}{48}
\end{aligned}
$$

Example: If $\mathrm{p}=-5$ and $\mathrm{q}=2$, find the value of $\mathrm{p}^{2} \mathrm{q}+p \mathrm{q}^{2}+2 \mathrm{p} \cdot \mathrm{pq}$
Solution: $\quad p^{2} q+p q^{2}+2 p \cdot p q$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\mathrm{p}^{2} \mathrm{q}+\mathrm{pq}^{2}+2 \mathrm{p}^{2} \mathrm{q} \quad \leftarrow \text { simplify the given expression } \\
& =3 \mathrm{p}^{2} \mathrm{q}+\mathrm{pq}^{2} \\
& =3(-5)^{2}(2)+(-5)(2)^{2} \leftarrow \text { combine like terms to reduce number of terms } \\
& =3(25)(2)+(-5)(4) \quad \leftarrow \text { simplify exponents } \\
& =150-20=130
\end{aligned}
$$

Example: Find the value of the expression

$$
3.1 x^{4}-0.2 x^{3} y^{2}+0.3 x^{2} y^{2}+1.1 x y^{3}-y^{4} \text { at } x=1, y=-1 .
$$

Solution: $3.1 x^{4}-0.2 x^{3} y^{2}+0.3 x^{2} y^{2}+1.1 x y^{3}-y^{4}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =3.1(1)^{4}-0.2(1)^{3}(-1)^{2}+0.3(1)^{2}(-1)^{2}+1.1(1)(-1)^{3}-(-1)^{4} \\
& =3.1-0.2(1)(+1)+0.3(1)(+1)+1.1(1)(-1)-(+1) \\
& =3.1-0.2+0.3-1.1-1 \\
& =3.4-2.3=1.1
\end{aligned}
$$

# 5.4 Basic Operations on Algebraic Fractions

## Basic Operations on Algebraic Fractions

Operations with rational numbers and rational algebraic expressions are very similar. A rational number can be expressed as the quotient of two integers. A rational algebraic expression can be expressed as the quotient of two polynomials. In either case, the denominator can never be 0 .
Rational Numbers: $\quad \frac{3}{11}, \frac{27}{100}, \frac{-7}{25}, \frac{61}{-89}$
Rational Algebraic Expressions: $\frac{5}{x}, \frac{y}{10}, \frac{2 x}{x-5}, \frac{y^{2}-36}{y+6}, \frac{x^{2}-6 x+5}{\frac{5 x}{x+1}}$
If $P$ and $Q$ are any two algebraic expressions then $\frac{P}{Q}$ is called an algebraic fraction, where $\mathrm{Q} \neq 0$ e.g., $\frac{a-b}{a^{2}+2 a b+b^{2}}$

To write a fraction in simplest form, we divide both the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor (GCD).

## Addition of Algebraic Expressions

Algebraic expressions can be added as explained in the following examples.
Example: Add the following expressions.
(i) $\frac{x^{2}-1}{2}, \frac{4\left(2 x^{2}-x+1\right)}{3}$
(ii) $\quad 4 x+7, \frac{(x+3)(x-1)}{x-2}$

## Solution:

(i) $\frac{x^{2}-1}{2}+\frac{4\left(2 x^{2}-x+1\right)}{3}$

## Kev Fact:

For all rational expressions
$\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}, b \neq 0, d \neq 0$ :
$\frac{a}{b}+\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a d+b c}{b d}, \frac{a}{b}-\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a d-b c}{b d}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{3\left(x^{2}-1\right)+8\left(2 x^{2}-x+1\right)}{6} \\
& =\frac{3 x^{2}-3+16 x^{2}-8 x+8}{6} \\
& =\frac{19 x^{2}-8 x+5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

(ii) $\quad 4 x+7+\frac{(x+3)(x-1)}{x-2}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{(4 x+7)(x-2)+(x+3)(x-1)}{x-2} \\
& =\frac{4 x^{2}+7 x-8 x-14+x^{2}+3 x-x-3}{x-2} \\
& =\frac{5 x^{2}+x-17}{x-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Subtraction of Algebraic Expressions

In subtraction of algebraic expressions, we have to change the signs of the expression which is being subtracted and all other steps are the same as in addition of algebraic expressions.
Example: Subtract $\frac{3 x^{2}-7 x+4}{x^{2}-4}$ from $\frac{3 x-1}{x+2}$.
Solution: $\frac{3 x-1}{x+2}-\frac{3 x^{2}-7 x+4}{x^{2}-4}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{3 x-1}{x+2}-\frac{3 x^{2}-7 x+4}{(x+2)(x-2)}-\text { By using the formula } \mathrm{a}^{2}-\mathrm{b}^{2}=(\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b})(\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{b}) \\
& =\frac{(3 x-1)(x-2)-\left(3 x^{2}-7 x+4\right)}{(x+2)(x-2)} \\
& =\frac{3 x^{2}-7 x+2-3 x^{2}+7 x-4}{(x+2)(x-2)}=\frac{-2}{(x+2)(x-2)}=\frac{-2}{x^{2}-4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplication of Algebraic Expressions

Multiplication of algebraic expression can be explained with the help of following examples.
Example: Find the product of $\frac{x^{2}-9}{x^{2}+2 x}$ and $\frac{x}{x+3}$.
Solution: $\frac{x^{2}-9}{x^{2}+2 x} \times \frac{x}{x+3}$

$$
=\frac{(x+3)(x-3)}{x(x+2)} \times \frac{x}{x+3}=\frac{x-3}{x+2}
$$

Example: Multiply $x^{2}-x-20$ by $\frac{3}{x^{3}+4 x^{2}}$.
Solution: $\left(x^{2}-x-20\right) \times \frac{3}{x^{3}+4 x^{2}}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\left(x^{2}-5 x+4 x-20\right) \times \frac{3}{x^{2}(x+4)} \\
& =(x-5)(x+4) \times \frac{3}{x^{2}(x+4)}=\frac{3 x-15}{x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Kev Fact:

For all rational expressions $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}, b \neq 0, d \neq 0$ :
$\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d}=\frac{a c}{b d}, \quad \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}=\frac{a d}{b c}$

## Division of Algebraic Expressions

The process of dividing any two algebraic expressions is explained with the help of following examples.
Example: Divide $\frac{2 x^{2}-11 x-21}{x^{2}-49}$ by $\frac{2 x+3}{x+7}$.
Solution: $\frac{2 x^{2}-11 x-21}{x^{2}-49} \div \frac{2 x+3}{x+7}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{2 x^{2}-14 x-3 x-21}{(x)^{2}-(7)^{2}} \times \frac{x+7}{2 x+3} \\
& =\frac{2 x(x-7)+3(x-7)}{(x+7)(x-7)} \times \frac{x+7}{2 x+3} \\
& =\frac{(x-7)(2 x+3)}{(x+7)(x-7)} \times \frac{x+7}{2 x+3}=1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Complex Algebraic Expressions

For simplifying the complex algebraic expression involving addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, we apply the BODMAS rule similar to arithmetic expression.

Example: Simplify.

$$
\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-2}{2 x+10} \div \frac{2 x^{2}-4}{x^{2}+2 x-15}
$$

Solution: $\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-2}{2 x+10} \times \frac{x^{2}+2 x-15}{2 x^{2}-8}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-2}{2(x+5)} \times \frac{x^{2}+5 x-3 x-15}{2\left(x^{2}-4\right)} \\
& =\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-2}{2(x+5)} \times \frac{x(x+5)-3(x+5)}{2(x+2)(x-2)} \\
& =\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-2}{2(x+5)} \times \frac{(x+5)(x-3)}{2(x+2)(x-2)} \\
& =\frac{3 x+2}{x+2}+\frac{x-3}{4(x+2)} \\
& =\frac{4(3 x+2)+(x-3)}{4(x+2)}=\frac{12 x+8+x-3}{4(x+2)}=\frac{13 x+5}{4 x+8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Example: Simplify the following.

$$
\left[\frac{a}{(a+b)^{2}-2 a b} \div \frac{(a+b)^{3}-3 a b(a+b)}{a^{4}-b^{4}}\right] \div \frac{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b}
$$

## Solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}

= & {\left[\frac{a}{a^{2}+b^{2}+2 a b-2 a b} \times \frac{\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{a^{3}+b^{3}+3 a b(a+b)-3 a b(a+b)}\right] \div \frac{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b} } \\
= & {\left[\frac{a}{a^{2}+b^{2}} \times \frac{\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{a^{3}+b^{3}}\right] \div \frac{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b} } \\
= & {\left[\frac{a}{a^{2}+b^{2}} \times \frac{(a+b)(a-b)\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{(a+b)\left(a^{2}-a b+b^{2}\right)}\right] \div \frac{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b} } \\
= & {\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{a^{2}-a b+b^{2}} \div \frac{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b}\right] }
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{a^{2}-a b+b^{2}} \times \frac{(a+b)^{2}-3 a b}{(a+b)^{2}-4 a b}\right] \\
& =\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{a^{2}-a b+b^{2}} \times \frac{a^{2}+2 a b+b^{2}-3 a b}{a^{2}+2 a b+b^{2}-4 a b}\right] \\
& =\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{a^{2}-a b+b^{2}} \times \frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-a b}{a^{2}+b^{2}-2 a b}\right] \\
& =\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{a^{2}-2 a b+b^{2}}\right] \\
& =\left[\frac{a(a-b)}{(a-b)^{2}}\right] \\
& =\frac{a}{a-b}
\end{aligned}
$$

# 5.5 Solving Rational Equations

## Solving Rational Equations

An equation that contains one or more rational expressions is called a rational equation. Here are some examples:

$$ \frac{2}{5}-\frac{x}{4}=\frac{1}{x}, \quad \frac{x-4}{x+1}-\frac{6}{x-2}=\frac{7}{x+1}, \quad x^{2}+\frac{5}{x}=9 $$

Equations of this type occur frequently in applications. It is easiest to solve a rational equation if the fractions are eliminated. This can be done by multiplying each side of the equation by the least common denominator (LCD). Remember that when you multiply each side by the LCD, each term on each side must be multiplied by the LCD. Example: Solve $\frac{3}{x-7}+1=\frac{8}{x^{2}-9 x+14}$. Verify the solution. Solution: $\quad \frac{3}{x-7}+1=\frac{8}{x^{2}-9 x+14}$

$$ \begin{aligned} (x-2)(x-7) \cdot \frac{3}{x-7}+(x-2)(x-7) \cdot 1 & =(x-2)(x-7) \cdot \frac{8}{(x-2)(x-7)} \text { Multiply by LCD }(x-2)(x-7) \\ 3(x-2)+\left(x^{2}-9 x+14\right) & =8 \text { Simplify. } \\ x^{2}-6 x+8 & =8 \text { Combine the like terms. } \\ x^{2}-6 x & =0 \text { Simplify. } \\ x(x-6) & =0 \end{aligned} $$

$x=0$, or $x=6$. The solutions are 0 and 6 . Check: if $x=0, \frac{3}{0-7}+1=\frac{8}{0^{2}-9.0+14}$, we have: $\frac{4}{7}=\frac{4}{7}$, True. if $x=6, \frac{3}{6-7}+1=\frac{8}{6^{2}-9.6+14}$, we have: $-2=-2$, True.

$$ S . S=\{0,6\} $$

Note: Remember that a rational expression is undefined when the value for variables results in a denominator of zero. When solving rational equations, we check that for solution denominator should be non-zero. If denominator is zero in any value of $x$, these values be excluded from list of solutions.
