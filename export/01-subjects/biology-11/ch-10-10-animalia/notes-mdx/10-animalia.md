# 10.1 Criteria for Animal Classification

# 10.1 Criteria for Animal Classification

Animals are classified using several key criteria that reflect their structural organization and evolutionary relationships. The four main criteria used in animal classification are:

1. **Presence or absence of true tissues** (Tissue Organization)
2. **Number of embryonic tissue (germ) layers**
3. **Type of body symmetry**
4. **Type of body cavity (coelom)**

---

## 1. Tissue Organization

Animals are first divided based on whether they possess true tissues:

- **Sub-kingdom Parazoa** (e.g., Sponges / Porifera): Lack true tissues and organs. Cells are loosely organized.
- **Sub-kingdom Eumetazoa**: Possess well-defined tissues, organs, and organ systems.

---

## 2. Number of Germ Layers

During embryonic development, animals form primary germ layers that give rise to all body tissues:

### Diploblastic Animals
- Body formed from **two germ layers**: **ectoderm** (outer) and **endoderm** (inner).
- A non-cellular **mesoglea** lies between the two layers.
- Example: **Cnidaria** (jellyfish, hydra).

### Triploblastic Animals
- Body formed from **three germ layers**: **ectoderm**, **mesoderm** (middle), and **endoderm**.
- The **mesoderm** gives rise to muscles, bones, and the circulatory system.
- All higher animals (Platyhelminthes and above) are triploblastic.

---

## 3. Body Symmetry

Symmetry describes how the body parts are arranged around a central point or axis:

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Asymmetry** | No symmetry; irregular body shape | Sponges (Porifera) |
| **Radial Symmetry** | Body parts arranged around a central axis; any plane through the centre divides the body into equal halves | Cnidaria, Echinoderms |
| **Bilateral Symmetry** | Only **one plane** divides the body into two mirror-image halves (left and right) | Most animals (Platyhelminthes, Arthropods, Chordates) |

**Bilateral symmetry** is an adaptation for **directional movement (motility)** and is associated with **cephalization** ,  the concentration of sensory organs and nervous tissue at the anterior (head) end.

---

## 4. Type of Body Cavity (Coelom)

The body cavity (coelom) is a fluid-filled space between the body wall and internal organs. Animals are classified into three groups:

### Acoelomate
- **No body cavity**; space between body wall and gut is filled with mesoderm (parenchyma).
- Example: **Platyhelminthes** (flatworms).

### Pseudocoelomate
- Possess a **false coelom (pseudocoelom)** located between the **mesoderm and endoderm**.
- The cavity is **not completely lined by mesoderm**.
- Example: **Aschelminthes / Nematoda** (roundworms).

### Coelomate (Eucoelomate)
- Possess a **true coelom** that is **completely lined by mesoderm** (peritoneum).
- Example: **Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Chordata**.

---

## 5. Protostomes vs. Deuterostomes (Further Classification of Coelomates)

Coelomate animals are further divided based on embryonic development:

| Feature | Protostomes | Deuterostomes |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| **Blastopore fate** | Becomes the **mouth** | Becomes the **anus** |
| **Cleavage type** | **Spiral** and determinate | **Radial** and indeterminate |
| **Coelom formation** | Schizocoelous | Enterocoelous |
| **Examples** | Molluscs, Annelids, Arthropods | Echinoderms, Chordates |

**Spiral cleavage**: Cells divide at oblique angles and sit in the furrows of lower cells.
**Radial cleavage**: Cells divide symmetrically and sit directly on top of one another.

---

# 10.2 Classification by Type of Body Cavity

# 10.2 Classification by Type of Body Cavity

One of the key criteria for classifying animals is the **type of body cavity** (coelom) present between the digestive tract and the outer body wall. On this basis, triploblastic animals are divided into three groups.

## What is a Coelom?

A **coelom** is a fluid-filled body cavity located between the digestive tract and the outer body wall. It is derived from the **mesoderm** (the middle embryonic germ layer). The coelom cushions internal organs, allows organ movement, and acts as a hydrostatic skeleton in some animals.

## Three Types of Body Cavity Organisation

### 1. Acoelomate
- Animals that **lack a body cavity** entirely.
- The space between the ectoderm and endoderm is packed with **solid mesodermal tissue** (mesenchyme).
- **Example:** Phylum Platyhelminthes (flatworms, e.g., *Planaria*, tapeworms).

### 2. Pseudocoelomate
- Animals with a **false body cavity** called a **pseudocoelom**.
- The pseudocoelom is located **between the mesoderm and endoderm** and is **not completely lined by mesoderm**.
- It is a persistent blastocoel (remnant of the embryonic cavity).
- **Example:** Phylum Aschelminthes / Nematoda (roundworms, e.g., *Ascaris*).

### 3. Coelomate (Eucoelomate)
- Animals with a **true coelom** that forms **within the mesoderm** and is **completely lined by mesoderm-derived tissue** called the **peritoneum**.
- The true coelom provides greater freedom of movement for internal organs.
- **Examples:** Phylum Annelida (earthworms), Phylum Mollusca, Phylum Arthropoda, Phylum Chordata.

## Comparison Table

| Feature | Acoelomate | Pseudocoelomate | Coelomate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body cavity | Absent | Present (false) | Present (true) |
| Lining | Solid mesoderm | Partially lined by mesoderm | Completely lined by mesoderm |
| Example | Platyhelminthes | Aschelminthes | Annelida, Chordata |

# 10.3 Diversity in Animals

# 10.3 Diversity in Animals

Animals show remarkable diversity in body plan, symmetry, and complexity. They are broadly divided into **invertebrates** (lacking a backbone) and **vertebrates** (possessing a vertebral column).

---

## Classification by Body Cavity

Animals are classified based on the presence and type of body cavity (coelom):

| Type | Body Cavity | Example Phyla |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoelomate** | No body cavity | Platyhelminthes |
| **Pseudocoelomate** | False coelom (not fully lined by mesoderm) | Aschelminthes (Nematoda) |
| **Coelomate** | True coelom (completely lined by mesoderm) | Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Chordata |

---

## Protostomes vs. Deuterostomes

Coelomate animals are further divided based on embryonic development:

### Protostomes
- Blastopore → **mouth** (first opening becomes the mouth)
- Cleavage: **spiral and determinate**
- Examples: Mollusca, Annelida, Arthropoda

### Deuterostomes
- Blastopore → **anus** (mouth forms secondarily)
- Cleavage: **radial and indeterminate**
- Examples: Echinodermata, Chordata

---

## Major Invertebrate Phyla

### Phylum Porifera (Sponges)
- Simplest multicellular animals; **no true tissues**
- Body perforated by pores (ostia) and canals
- Filter feeders; sessile
- Skeleton of **spicules** (silica or calcium carbonate) or spongin fibres
- Example: *Sycon*, *Spongilla*

### Phylum Cnidaria
- Diploblastic (ectoderm + endoderm)
- Radial symmetry
- Possess **nematocysts** (stinging cells) for defence and prey capture
- Two body forms: **polyp** (sessile) and **medusa** (free-swimming)
- Examples: *Hydra*, jellyfish, sea anemones, corals

### Phylum Platyhelminthes (Flatworms)
- **Acoelomate**, triploblastic
- Bilaterally symmetrical, dorsoventrally flattened
- No circulatory or respiratory system
- Examples: *Taenia* (tapeworm), *Fasciola* (liver fluke), *Planaria*

### Phylum Aschelminthes / Nematoda (Roundworms)
- **Pseudocoelomate**, triploblastic
- Cylindrical, unsegmented body with a complete digestive tract
- Many are parasitic
- Examples: *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria* (filarial worm)

### Phylum Annelida (Segmented Worms)
- **Coelomate**, triploblastic
- Body divided into **metameric segments**
- Closed circulatory system; excretion by nephridia
- Examples: *Lumbricus* (earthworm), *Nereis*, *Hirudo* (leech)

### Phylum Mollusca
- **Coelomate**; soft-bodied, usually with a shell
- Mantle secretes the shell; muscular foot for locomotion
- Open circulatory system (except cephalopods)
- Examples: *Helix* (snail), *Mytilus* (mussel), *Octopus*

### Phylum Arthropoda
- **Largest animal phylum** by number of species
- **Chitinous exoskeleton** and **jointed appendages**
- Segmented body; open circulatory system
- Classes: Insecta, Arachnida, Crustacea, Myriapoda
- Examples: insects, spiders, crabs, centipedes

### Phylum Echinodermata
- **Deuterostomes**; adults show **pentaradial symmetry**
- Unique **Water Vascular System (Ambulacral system)** ,  hydraulic system operating tube feet for locomotion and food capture
- Endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles
- Examples: starfish (*Asterias*), sea urchin, sea cucumber

---

## Phylum Chordata

Chordates are **deuterostomes** defined by four diagnostic features present at some stage of development:

1. **Notochord** ,  flexible rod providing support
2. **Dorsal hollow nerve cord** ,  develops into brain and spinal cord
3. **Pharyngeal gill slits** ,  openings in the pharynx
4. **Post-anal tail** ,  extension of body posterior to the anus

### Sub-phyla of Chordata

| Sub-phylum | Key Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Urochordata** (Tunicates) | Notochord only in larva | *Herdmania* |
| **Cephalochordata** (Lancelets) | Notochord persists throughout life | *Amphioxus* |
| **Vertebrata** | Notochord replaced by **vertebral column** | Fish, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia |

---

## Vertebrate Classes

### Class Chondrichthyes (Cartilaginous Fish)
- Skeleton entirely of **cartilage**
- No operculum; no swim bladder
- Placoid scales; internal fertilization
- Examples: sharks, rays

### Class Osteichthyes (Bony Fish)
- Bony skeleton; **operculum** covers gills
- Swim bladder for buoyancy
- Examples: *Labeo* (rohu), salmon, tuna

### Class Amphibia
- First tetrapods; moist, scaleless skin
- Larvae aquatic (breathe via gills); adults terrestrial (breathe via lungs and skin)
- Examples: frogs, toads, salamanders

### Class Reptilia
- Dry, scaly skin; ectothermic
- Amniotic egg with leathery shell
- Examples: lizards, snakes, crocodiles, turtles

### Class Aves (Birds)
- Endothermic; body covered with **feathers**
- Forelimbs modified into wings; hollow bones
- Examples: *Columba* (pigeon), eagle, penguin

### Class Mammalia
- Endothermic; body covered with **hair/fur**
- **Mammary glands** produce milk to nourish young
- Most are viviparous
- Examples: humans, whales, bats, dogs
