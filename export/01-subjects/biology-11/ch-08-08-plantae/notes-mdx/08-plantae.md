# 8.1 Overview of Kingdom Plantae

This section covers the evolutionary origins, diagnostic features, and major classifications within the plant kingdom, emphasizing the transition from aquatic to terrestrial life and the key adaptations involved.

## Origin and Evolution

Plants transitioned from aquatic environments to land approximately 400 million years ago.

**Closest Relatives:** Freshwater green algae, specifically Charophyceae (e.g., *Chara*), are identified as the closest living relatives to land plants.

**Evidence for Relationship with Charophyceae:**

- Both have a high percentage of cellulose in their cell walls. <InlineNoteTag label="Cell Wall" notePath="biology-11/5-cell-wall-and-plasma-membrane" />
- The flagellated sperm of some plants resembles that of charophycean sperm.
- Genetic comparisons of nuclear and chloroplast genes confirm a close relationship.

**Note:** Plants and Charophyceae evolved from a common ancestor (monophyletic lineage); Charophyceae are not the direct ancestors of plants.

## Diagnostic Features of Plants

Plants are multicellular eukaryotes with well-developed tissues and cellulose-rich cell walls. <InlineNoteTag label="Eukaryotic Cells" notePath="biology-11/9-prokaryotic-and-eukaryotic-cells" /> Their nutrition is autotrophic, primarily through photosynthesis, and their adaptation to land includes a waxy cuticle made of cutin that covers the plant, preventing desiccation (drying out). The body plan is typically organized into roots, stems, and leaves, supported by specialized vascular tissues for transport: xylem transports water and minerals, while phloem transports sugars (food).

## Alternation of Generations

Plants exhibit a life cycle that alternates between two distinct, multicellular generations: a diploid sporophyte and a haploid gametophyte. This is known as **heteromorphic alternation of generations** because the two generations are morphologically different.

The **sporophyte** is the diploid ($2n$) generation, and it produces haploid ($n$) spores through meiosis. <InlineNoteTag label="Meiosis" notePath="biology-11/12-cell-division" /> The **gametophyte** is the haploid ($n$) generation; it develops from a spore and produces gametes through mitosis in multicellular sex organs. Reproduction is oogamous, meaning the gametes are morphologically and physiologically different, for example a large, non-motile egg and a smaller, motile sperm. Fertilization, the fusion of gametes, forms a diploid zygote, which develops into a new sporophyte.

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002031123.webp" alt="Lifecycle of plants" caption="Figure 8.1: Lifecycle of plants showing heteromorphic alternation of generations" />

## Classification of Kingdom Plantae

The plant kingdom is broadly divided based on the presence or absence of vascular tissue.

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002031253.webp" alt="Phylogeny in Kingdom Plantae" caption="Figure 8.2: Phylogeny in Kingdom Plantae" />

| Group | Key Characteristic | Sub-groups / Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bryophytes** | Non-vascular plants (lack true xylem and phloem) | Liverworts, Mosses, Hornworts |
| **Tracheophytes** | Vascular plants (possess true xylem and phloem) | Includes Lycophytes and Euphyllophytes |

**Further Division of Tracheophytes:**

- **Pteridophytes:** Free-sporing vascular plants. This is a paraphyletic group (doesn't include all descendants of a common ancestor).
  - **Lycophytes:** Clubmosses and relatives.
  - **Euphyllophytes (partially):** Ferns, horsetails.
- **Spermatophytes:** Seed-producing vascular plants. This is a monophyletic group.
  - **Gymnosperms:** "Naked seed" plants (non-flowering). Examples: Conifers, cycads.
  - **Angiosperms:** Flowering plants; seeds are enclosed within a fruit.

# Bryophyta (Non-Vascular Plants)

Bryophytes are a division of plants that lack true vascular tissues (xylem and phloem). They represent an evolutionary transition from aquatic to terrestrial life and include mosses, liverworts, and hornworts. They do not have true roots, stems, or leaves, but possess analogous structures often referred to as root-like, stem-like, and leaf-like.

## General Characteristics of Bryophytes

Bryophytes are non-vascular: they lack specialized tissues (xylem and phloem) for transporting water and nutrients, and are typically small, rarely exceeding 2 cm in length. They are primarily found in moist, wet environments, though some are adapted to dry conditions. Water is essential for their reproduction, as the male gametes (*sperms*) are motile and must swim to reach the egg, a dependency on water that gives them the name "amphibious plants."

The main plant body is the *gametophyte* (haploid, $n$), which is green, photosynthetic, and the most conspicuous phase of the life cycle; the *sporophyte* (diploid, $2n$) is smaller, not free-living, and nutritionally dependent on the gametophyte, turning brownish or straw-colored at maturity. The sporophyte produces only one type of spore, so bryophytes are homosporous, and they possess multicellular sex organs (antheridia and archegonia) which produce an embryo.

## Land Adaptations in Bryophytes

Bryophytes have developed several key adaptations to survive in terrestrial habitats:

1. **Compact Multicellular Body:** Reduces the surface area exposed to dry air, minimizing water loss. A *cuticle* further prevents evaporation.
2. **Photosynthetic Chambers:** Specialized internal chambers allow for efficient CO₂ absorption while minimizing water loss.

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002031335.webp" alt="Marchantia thallus section" caption="Fig: 8.3: Transverse section of Marchantia thallus showing photosynthetic chambers." />

3. **Rhizoids:** Root-like structures that provide anchorage and aid in water absorption.
4. **Heterogamy (Oogamy):** Production of two distinct gametes: a large, non-motile egg with stored food and small, motile sperm.
5. **Protected Gametes:** Multicellular sex organs, the *antheridium* (male) and *archegonium* (female), protect the gametes during development.
6. **Protected Embryo:** The multicellular embryo is retained and nourished within the female archegonium.
7. **Alternation of Generations:** The life cycle alternates between a haploid gametophyte and a diploid sporophyte, allowing for genetic variation and adaptation.

## Classification of Bryophytes

Bryophytes are divided into three main subdivisions:

- **Hepaticopsida:** Liverworts
- **Bryopsida:** Mosses
- **Anthoceropsida:** Hornworts

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002031402.webp" alt="Liverworts, Mosses, Hornworts" caption="Fig: 8.4: Liverworts, Mosses, Hornworts." />

## Life Cycle of *Polytrichum* (A Moss)

The life cycle demonstrates a clear **alternation of generations**.

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002031513.webp" alt="Lifecycle of Polytrichum" caption="Fig: 8.5: Lifecycle of Polytrichum (a moss plant)" />

### 1. Gametophytic Generation ($n$)

- **Main Plant Body:** The visible "leafy" plant, with an erect stem-like structure, leaf-like appendages, and rhizoids for anchorage. It is photosynthetic.
- **Sex Organs:** Produced at the apex of the leafy shoot.
    - **Antheridium (Male):** A multicellular, stalked capsule that produces numerous biflagellated sperm (*antherozoids*) by mitosis. It is protected by a sterile jacket layer.
    - **Archegonium (Female):** A flask-shaped organ with a swollen base (*venter*) containing a single egg (*ovum*) and a long *neck*. The egg is produced by mitosis.
- **Paraphyses:** Sterile, multicellular hair-like structures intermixed with the sex organs.
- ***Polytrichum*** is **dioecious**, meaning antheridia and archegonia are found on separate male and female plants.

### 2. Fertilization

- The antheridium ruptures, releasing sperm.
- Sperm require a thin film of water to swim to the archegonium.
- Cells in the archegonial neck degrade to form a mucilaginous passage, guiding the sperm to the egg.
- Fertilization occurs within the archegonium, forming a **diploid ($2n$) zygote**.

### 3. Sporophytic Generation ($2n$)

The **zygote** is the first cell of the sporophyte generation. It undergoes mitosis to develop into a multicellular **embryo** within the archegonium, and the embryo matures into the **sporophyte**, which remains attached to and dependent on the gametophyte. The sporophyte has three parts: a **foot** embedded in the gametophyte tissue that absorbs nutrients, a **seta** (a long stalk), and a **capsule** (sporangium), the spore-producing sac at the tip of the seta. Inside the capsule, **meiosis** occurs to produce numerous **haploid ($n$) spores**.

### 4. Germination of Spores

- The **spore** is the first cell of the new gametophyte generation.
- Upon germination, it grows into a filamentous, alga-like structure called the **protonema**.
- Buds form on the protonema, which then develop into the mature, leafy gametophyte plant.

### Comparison of Generations

| Feature | Gametophyte Generation | Sporophyte Generation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ploidy** | Haploid ($n$) | Diploid ($2n$) |
| **Dominance** | Dominant, independent, photosynthetic | Recessive, dependent on gametophyte |
| **Main Structures** | Leafy shoot, rhizoids, antheridia, archegonia | Foot, seta, capsule (sporangium) |
| **First Cell** | Spore | Zygote |
| **Produces** | Gametes (by mitosis) | Spores (by meiosis) |

# TRACHEOPHYTA (VASCULAR PLANTS)

# TRACHEOPHYTA (VASCULAR PLANTS)

Traacheophytes are **vascular plants**, defined by the presence of specialized conductive tissues: **xylem** for water and minerals, and **phloem** for organic solutes. Their name is derived from the universal presence of **tracheids** (a type of water-conducting cell) in their xylem.

## General Characteristics of Tracheophytes

Traacheophytes represent the most successful and dominant land plants. Their success is attributed to several key evolutionary adaptations:

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Dominant Sporophyte** | The main plant body is the diploid (2n) sporophyte, differentiated into true roots, stems, and leaves |
| **Vascular System** | Xylem and phloem in all major organs enable efficient transport, allowing larger size and colonization of diverse habitats |
| **Protected Sporangia** | Protected spore-producing structures; this trend ultimately led to the **seed** |
| **Pollen Tube** | Allows water-independent fertilization ,  male gametes reach female gametes without external water |
| **Flowers & Fruits** | Flowers attract pollinators; fruits aid seed dispersal (in angiosperms) |
| **Heteromorphic Alternation of Generations** | Large dominant sporophyte + small reduced gametophyte; provides environmental flexibility |
| **Lignin** | Deposited in cell walls for structural support, enabling upright growth |

## Classification of Tracheophyta

Traacheophytes are classified into **four main subdivisions**:

| Subdivision | Common Name | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| *Psilopsida* | Whisk ferns | Most primitive; no true roots or leaves |
| *Lycopsida* | Club mosses | Microphylls; includes *Selaginella* |
| *Sphenopsida* | Horsetails | Jointed, ribbed stems; includes *Equisetum* |
| *Pteropsida* | Ferns & seed plants | Megaphylls; most diverse group |

## Evolution of Leaves

The evolution of vascular plants is marked by significant changes in leaf morphology:

### Microphylls
- Small leaves with a **single, unbranched vascular strand**
- Found in **Lycopsids** (e.g., *Selaginella*, *Lycopodium*)
- Thought to have evolved from surface outgrowths (enations) of the stem

### Megaphylls
- Large leaves with a **complex, branched network of veins**
- Found in **ferns and seed plants** (Pteropsida)
- Evolution explained by the **Telome Theory**, involving three processes:
  1. **Overtopping** ,  one branch of a dichotomous fork grows faster and dominates
  2. **Planation** ,  branches flatten into a single plane
  3. **Webbing (Syngenesis)** ,  photosynthetic tissue fills the spaces between flattened branches

## Evolution of Seeds: Heterospory

The transition from **homospory** (one type of spore) to **heterospory** (two types of spores) was a critical prerequisite for seed development:

- **Microspores** → develop into male gametophytes (pollen)
- **Megaspores** → develop into female gametophytes (retained within the megasporangium in seed plants)

Retention of the megaspore within the megasporangium, surrounded by an **integument**, ultimately gave rise to the **ovule** and then the **seed**.

## Specific Adaptations in Pteropsida

Pteropsida includes **ferns, gymnosperms, and angiosperms** ,  the most diverse and ecologically dominant vascular plants.

**Ferns** (seedless Pteropsida) exhibit:
- **Circinate vernation** ,  young leaves (fronds) are tightly coiled (fiddlehead shape) and unroll from base to tip as they mature
- **Sori** ,  clusters of sporangia on the underside of fronds
- **Prothallus** ,  the small, heart-shaped, independent gametophyte generation

# 8.4 Pteridophyta: Seedless Vascular Plants

Pteridophytes are the first true land plants possessing vascular tissues (xylem and phloem) but lacking seeds. They represent a significant evolutionary step from bryophytes. This group includes four main sub-divisions: Psilopsida, Lycopsida, Sphenopsida, and Pteropsida (of which the class Fillicinae, or ferns, are a part).

## General Characteristics of Pteridophytes

Pteridophytes are *tracheophytes*, meaning they possess vascular tissues (xylem and phloem) for the transport of water and nutrients. They are seedless and reproduce via spores. Their life cycle exhibits a distinct *heteromorphic alternation of generations*, where the diploid sporophyte and haploid gametophyte are morphologically different, and the **sporophyte generation is dominant**. They are typically found in moist, shaded environments, as water is required for fertilization.

## Sub-Divisions of Pteridophyta

### 1. Psilopsida (Whisk Ferns)

* **Structure:** Simplest vascular plants. They lack true roots and leaves.
    * ***Rhizome:*** An underground stem for anchorage, possessing *rhizoids*.
    * ***Aerial Stems:*** Green, photosynthetic stems with small scale-like appendages.
* **Reproduction:** *Sporangia* (spore-producing structures) are located at the tips or on the sides of aerial branches.
* **Gametophyte:** A colorless, subterranean structure that lives in a symbiotic relationship with a fungus (*mycorrhiza*) for nutrition.
* **Examples:** *Psilotum* (living), *Rhynia* (extinct).

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002185734.webp" alt="Rhynia" caption="Figure 8.6: Rhynia" />

### 2. Lycopsida (Club Mosses, Spike Mosses, Quillworts)

* **Structure:** Plant body has a branching rhizome with aerial stems.
    * ***Microphylls:*** Stems and branches are covered in small, scale-like leaves, each with a single, unbranched vein of vascular tissue.
* **Reproduction:** Sporangia are borne on terminal clusters of leaves called ***strobili*** (cones), which have a club-shaped appearance.
* **Gametophyte:** Mostly an underground structure.
* **Examples:** *Lycopodium* (Club moss), *Selaginella* (Spike moss).

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002185844.webp" alt="Lycopodium" caption="Figure 8.7: Lycopodium" />

### 3. Sphenopsida (Horsetails)

* **Structure:** Also known as *arthrophytes* due to their jointed stems.
    * ***Rhizome:*** Gives rise to two types of aerial stems: fertile and sterile.
    * ***Aerial Stems:*** Slender, green, hollow, and jointed.
    * ***Leaves:*** Whorls of scale-like leaves are present only at the nodes (joints). Photosynthesis occurs mainly in the stem.
* **Reproduction:** Spores are produced in *cones* (strobili) located at the tips of fertile shoots.
* **Gametophyte:** A small, thalloid structure that grows on moist soil.
* **Example:** *Equisetum* is the only surviving genus.

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002185945.webp" alt="Horsetail, Equisetum" caption="Figure 8.8: Horsetail, Equisetum" />

### 4. Pteropsida - Class Fillicinae (Ferns)

* **General:** The most abundant and diverse group of seedless vascular plants, thriving in warm, moist tropics.
* **Size:** Varies from tiny aquatic forms to large tree ferns.
* **Dominance:** The **sporophyte generation** is large, complex, and independent.
* **Leaves (Fronds):** Large, complex leaves (megaphylls) that serve both photosynthesis and reproduction. Young fronds exhibit ***circinate vernation*** (coiled like a fiddlehead).

## Life Cycle of a Fern (e.g., Adiantum)

<CaptionedImage src="/content/assets/class-11/biology/Pasted image 20251002190029.webp" alt="Life cycle of fern (Adiantum)" caption="Figure 8.10: Life cycle of fern (Adiantum)" />

### Sporophyte Generation (2n)

This is the main, recognizable fern plant. Its structure includes a **rhizome**, an underground, horizontal stem covered in brown scales called *ramenta*; **roots**, adventitious roots that arise from the rhizome; and **fronds (leaves)**, large, pinnately compound leaves whose main stalk is the *rachis*, with leaflets called *pinnae* (1st order) and *pinnules* (2nd order). Reproduction centers on **sori**, clusters of sporangia found on the underside of leaflets, often protected by a bent leaflet margin (*false-indusium*). Each **sporangium** is a capsule on a stalk containing spore mother cells, with a wall bearing a specialized ring of cells (*annulus*) and a weak point (*stomium*). Spore mother cells undergo **meiosis** to produce haploid (n) spores, and the annulus contracts as it dries during spore dispersal, tearing the sporangium at the stomium and flinging the spores out.

### Gametophyte Generation (n)

* A germinating spore grows into the gametophyte, also called a **prothallus**.
* **Structure:**
    * A small, heart-shaped, green, photosynthetic structure.
    * It is independent and anchors itself with *rhizoids*.
* **Reproductive Organs (on the same prothallus):**
    * ***Antheridia:*** Globular structures that produce motile, multi-ciliated sperm (*antherozoids*).
    * ***Archegonia:*** Flask-shaped structures containing a single egg (*oosphere*).

### Fertilization

1. Water is essential for fertilization.
2. Antherozoids are released and swim towards the archegonia, attracted by chemical signals (*chemotaxis*).
3. One antherozoid fertilizes the egg, forming a diploid (2n) **zygote**.
4. The zygote develops into an embryo, which initially depends on the gametophyte but quickly grows into an independent **sporophyte**, completing the cycle.

## Summary

* Pteridophytes are the first vascular plants, but they are seedless and rely on spores for reproduction.
* The life cycle is an alternation of generations, with the **diploid sporophyte** being the dominant, long-lived phase.
* The **haploid gametophyte** (prothallus in ferns) is a small but independent, photosynthetic plant.
* Water is a limiting factor for reproduction, as it is required for the motile sperm to reach the egg.
* The four main groups show a progression in structural complexity.

| Group        | Common Name  | Key Distinguishing Features                                 | Example     |
|--------------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| **Psilopsida**  | Whisk Ferns  | No true roots or leaves; dichotomously branching stems.   | *Psilotum*  |
| **Lycopsida**   | Club Mosses  | Have microphylls (leaves with a single vein); spores in strobili. | *Lycopodium*|
| **Sphenopsida** | Horsetails   | Jointed, hollow, photosynthetic stems; scale-like leaves in whorls. | *Equisetum* |
| **Fillicinae**  | Ferns        | Large leaves (fronds) with complex venation; circinate vernation. | *Adiantum*  |

**Biological Significance:** Pteridophytes represent a crucial evolutionary link between non-vascular bryophytes and seed-bearing plants. Their development of vascular tissue was a key adaptation for life on land, allowing them to grow taller and colonize more diverse habitats.

# 8.5 Spermatophyta (Seed-Bearing Plants)

# 8.5 Spermatophyta (Seed-Bearing Plants)

Spermatophytes are the most advanced and dominant group of vascular plants, characterized by the production of **seeds**. They include two major groups: **Gymnosperms** and **Angiosperms**.

---

## Evolutionary Prerequisites for the Seed Habit

Before seeds could evolve, several key adaptations were necessary:

1. **Heterospory:** Production of two distinct spore types ,  small **microspores** (male) and large **megaspores** (female). This leads to differentiation of male and female gametophytes.
2. **Retention of the megaspore:** The megaspore is permanently retained within the megasporangium (nucellus) rather than being shed ,  this is the ovule.
3. **Integuments:** Protective layers surrounding the megasporangium that develop into the **seed coat (testa)** after fertilization.
4. **Pollen tube (Siphonogamy):** Delivers non-motile male gametes to the egg without requiring external water, enabling life in dry habitats.

---

## Division I: Gymnosperms

### General Characteristics (SLO B-11-B-14)

- **Naked seeds:** Ovules and seeds are borne directly on cone scales, **not enclosed** within an ovary/fruit.
- Mostly **evergreen trees and shrubs** with needle-like or scale-like leaves.
- Vascular tissue (xylem and phloem) present; xylem lacks vessels (tracheids only in most).
- **Dominant sporophyte** generation; gametophyte is microscopic and dependent.
- Fertilization via **pollen tube** (siphonogamy) ,  no water required.
- Produce both **male cones** (microsporangiate) and **female cones** (megasporangiate).

### Classification of Gymnosperms

| Division | Example | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Cycadophyta | *Cycas* | Palm-like, living fossils |
| Ginkgophyta | *Ginkgo biloba* | Fan-shaped leaves, living fossil |
| Coniferophyta | *Pinus*, *Cedrus* | Needle leaves, woody cones |
| Gnetophyta | *Ephedra* | Vessel elements in xylem |

---

## Life Cycle of *Pinus* (SLO B-11-B-15)

*Pinus* (pine tree) is the classic example of a gymnosperm life cycle. The **sporophyte** (the tree) is dominant.

### Male Cones (Microsporangiate Cones)
- Small, clustered at branch tips.
- Each scale bears two **microsporangia**.
- Microsporangia undergo **meiosis** → **microspores** → develop into **pollen grains** (immature male gametophyte).
- Mature pollen grain contains: 2 prothallial cells, 1 generative cell, 1 tube cell.
- Pollen is wind-dispersed (**anemophily**).

### Female Cones (Megasporangiate Cones)
- Large, woody, persistent.
- Each scale bears two **ovules**.
- Each ovule contains: **integuments** (2), **micropyle** (opening), **nucellus** (megasporangium).
- One cell in nucellus undergoes **meiosis** → 4 megaspores → 3 degenerate, 1 functional megaspore.
- Functional megaspore develops into **female gametophyte** (endosperm tissue + 2–3 **archegonia** each containing an egg).

### Pollination and Fertilization
1. Pollen grains enter through the **micropyle** of the ovule (wind pollination).
2. **Pollen tube** grows slowly through the nucellus (takes ~12–14 months in *Pinus*).
3. Generative cell divides → 2 sperm cells (non-motile).
4. One sperm fuses with the egg → **zygote** (2n) → **embryo**.
5. Integuments harden → **seed coat (testa)**.
6. Seed is released when cone scales open.

$$\text{Microspore} \xrightarrow{\text{mitosis}} \text{Pollen grain} \xrightarrow{\text{pollen tube}} \text{Sperm} + \text{Egg} \rightarrow \text{Zygote} \rightarrow \text{Embryo}$$

---

## Division II: Angiosperms (Flowering Plants)

### General Characteristics (SLO B-11-B-16)

- Seeds are **enclosed within a fruit** (ripened ovary wall ,  pericarp).
- Possess **flowers** as reproductive organs.
- **Double fertilization** (unique to Angiosperms).
- Dominant sporophyte; highly reduced gametophyte (male = 3-celled pollen grain; female = 7-celled embryo sac).
- Xylem contains **vessels** (efficient water transport).
- Most diverse group of plants (~300,000 species).

### Classification of Angiosperms

| Feature | Monocots | Dicots |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | 1 | 2 |
| Leaf venation | Parallel | Reticulate (net-like) |
| Root system | Fibrous | Tap root |
| Floral parts | Multiples of 3 | Multiples of 4 or 5 |
| Vascular bundles | Scattered | In a ring |
| Examples | Wheat, maize, grass | Rose, mango, bean |

---

## Life Cycle of Angiosperms (SLO B-11-B-17)

### Male Gametophyte (Pollen Grain)
- Anther contains **microsporangia** (pollen sacs).
- Microsporocytes undergo **meiosis** → **microspores** → each develops into a **pollen grain**.
- Mature pollen grain = **3 cells**: 1 tube cell + 2 sperm cells (generative cell divides).

### Female Gametophyte (Embryo Sac)
- Ovule contains **megasporangium (nucellus)**.
- One megasporocyte undergoes **meiosis** → 4 megaspores → 3 degenerate, 1 functional.
- Functional megaspore undergoes **3 mitotic divisions** → **7-celled, 8-nucleate embryo sac**:
  - 3 antipodal cells
  - 2 synergids
  - 1 egg cell
  - 1 central cell with 2 **polar nuclei**

### Pollination
- Transfer of pollen from anther to **stigma** of the same or different flower.
- Agents: wind (anemophily), insects (entomophily), water, birds.

### Double Fertilization (Unique to Angiosperms)
1. Pollen grain germinates on stigma → **pollen tube** grows through style to ovule.
2. Two sperm cells released:
   - **Sperm 1 + Egg cell** → **Zygote (2n)** → develops into **Embryo**
   - **Sperm 2 + 2 Polar nuclei** → **Primary Endosperm Nucleus (3n)** → develops into **Endosperm** (food reserve)

$$\text{Sperm}_1 + \text{Egg} \rightarrow \text{Zygote (2n)} \rightarrow \text{Embryo}$$
$$\text{Sperm}_2 + \text{Polar nuclei (2)} \rightarrow \text{PEN (3n)} \rightarrow \text{Endosperm}$$

### Seed and Fruit Formation
- Zygote → **embryo** (with cotyledons, plumule, radicle)
- Integuments → **seed coat (testa)**
- Ovary wall → **fruit (pericarp)**
- Ovule → **seed**

---

## Comparison: Gymnosperms vs. Angiosperms

| Feature | Gymnosperms | Angiosperms |
|---|---|---|
| Seeds | Naked (on cone scales) | Enclosed in fruit |
| Flowers | Absent | Present |
| Fertilization | Simple | Double |
| Endosperm | Haploid (pre-fertilization) | Triploid (3n) |
| Xylem | Tracheids only (mostly) | Vessels + tracheids |
| Fruit | Absent | Present |
| Examples | *Pinus*, *Cycas* | Rose, mango, wheat |
