# 11.1 Male Reproductive System

# 11.1 Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system consists of **primary organs** (gonads) and **accessory organs** that together produce, store, and deliver sperm.

---

## Primary Organs: The Testes

The **testes** are the male gonads, located outside the body cavity in the **scrotum**. The scrotum maintains a temperature approximately $2-3^\circ C$ lower than core body temperature ,  a requirement for viable spermatogenesis.

Each testis contains:

- **Seminiferous tubules** ,  highly coiled tubes where sperm are produced (spermatogenesis)
- **Interstitial cells (Leydig cells)** ,  located between the tubules; secrete **testosterone** under stimulation by LH (also called ICSH in males)
- **Sertoli cells (Nurse cells)** ,  line the seminiferous tubules; provide nourishment, a liquid medium, and protection to developing spermatids

---

## Accessory Organs

### Epididymis
A long, coiled tube resting on the posterior surface of each testis. Sperm undergo **functional maturation** here, gaining motility over approximately 2–4 weeks. Sperm are also stored in the epididymis.

### Vas Deferens (Ductus Deferens)
A muscular tube that carries mature sperm from the epididymis toward the ejaculatory duct during ejaculation.

### Ejaculatory Duct
Formed by the union of the vas deferens and the duct of the seminal vesicle. It passes through the prostate gland and opens into the urethra.

### Urethra
The final common pathway for both urine and semen. During ejaculation, a sphincter prevents urine from mixing with semen.

---

## Accessory Glands

| Gland | Secretion | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Seminal Vesicles** | Thick, alkaline fluid (~60% of semen) | Contains **fructose** (energy for sperm), **prostaglandins** (stimulate uterine contractions), and clotting proteins |
| **Prostate Gland** | Milky, slightly acidic fluid (~30% of semen) | Contains **citrate**, zinc, and enzymes that activate and nourish sperm |
| **Bulbourethral Glands (Cowper's Glands)** | Clear, alkaline mucus | Neutralises residual urine acidity in the urethra before ejaculation; provides lubrication |

---

## Male Reproductive Hormones

The male reproductive system is regulated by a hormonal axis:

1. **Hypothalamus** → releases **GnRH** (Gonadotropin Releasing Hormone)
2. **Anterior Pituitary** → releases:
   - **LH (ICSH)** → stimulates Leydig cells → **Testosterone** secretion
   - **FSH** → acts on Sertoli cells → supports **spermatogenesis**
3. **Testosterone** → negative feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary to regulate its own levels

### Functions of Testosterone
- Development of **primary sexual characteristics** (testes, penis)
- Development of **secondary sexual characteristics** (facial hair, deepening voice, increased muscle mass, bone density)
- Maintenance of spermatogenesis
- Stimulates protein anabolism

---

## Path of Sperm: Summary

$$\text{Seminiferous Tubules} \rightarrow \text{Rete Testis} \rightarrow \text{Epididymis} \rightarrow \text{Vas Deferens} \rightarrow \text{Ejaculatory Duct} \rightarrow \text{Urethra}$$

> **Semen** = sperm + secretions from seminal vesicles + prostate + bulbourethral glands

# 11.2 Female Reproductive Cycle and Hormonal Regulation

# 11.2 Female Reproductive Cycle and Hormonal Regulation

The **menstrual cycle** is a recurring monthly cycle of hormonal and physiological changes in the female reproductive system that prepares the uterus for potential pregnancy. It averages **28 days** and is regulated by a coordinated interplay of hormones from the hypothalamus, anterior pituitary, and ovaries.

---

## Phases of the Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is divided into **ovarian** and **uterine** phases that occur simultaneously.

### Ovarian Cycle

| Phase | Days | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| Follicular Phase | 1–13 | FSH stimulates follicle development; estrogen rises |
| Ovulation | ~Day 14 | LH surge causes Graafian follicle to rupture |
| Luteal Phase | 15–28 | Corpus luteum secretes progesterone |

### Uterine Cycle

| Phase | Days | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| Menstrual Phase | 1–5 | Endometrium sheds due to low progesterone |
| Proliferative Phase | 6–13 | Estrogen rebuilds endometrium |
| Secretory Phase | 15–28 | Progesterone prepares endometrium for implantation |

---

## Hormones and Their Roles

### 1. Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH)
- Secreted by the **hypothalamus**
- Stimulates the anterior pituitary to release FSH and LH

### 2. Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
- Secreted by the **anterior pituitary**
- Stimulates development of **primary follicles** into **Graafian follicles**
- Induces follicular cells to secrete **estrogen**

### 3. Estrogen
- Secreted by **growing follicles**
- Causes **proliferation** (repair and thickening) of the uterine endometrium
- At high levels, triggers a **positive feedback** surge of LH
- Exerts **negative feedback** on FSH/LH at moderate levels

### 4. Luteinizing Hormone (LH)
- Secreted by the **anterior pituitary**
- The **LH surge** (~day 14) triggers **ovulation** ,  rupture of the Graafian follicle
- Stimulates the ruptured follicle to form the **corpus luteum**

### 5. Progesterone
- Secreted by the **corpus luteum**
- Maintains the **secretory endometrium** for potential implantation
- Exerts **negative feedback** on the anterior pituitary, inhibiting FSH and LH
- If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates → progesterone drops → menstruation begins

---

## Key Structures

### Graafian Follicle
A mature ovarian follicle containing the secondary oocyte, surrounded by follicular cells that produce estrogen. It ruptures at ovulation.

### Corpus Luteum
A **yellowish glandular structure** formed from the ruptured Graafian follicle after ovulation. It is the primary source of **progesterone** during the luteal phase. If fertilization does not occur, it degenerates into the **corpus albicans** by day 28.

---

## Hormonal Feedback Mechanisms

### Negative Feedback
- **Moderate estrogen + progesterone** → inhibit GnRH, FSH, and LH → prevent new follicle development during the luteal phase

### Positive Feedback (LH Surge)
- **High estrogen** (just before ovulation) → stimulates a massive LH surge → triggers ovulation

---

## Summary: Hormonal Timeline

$$\text{GnRH} \rightarrow \text{FSH} \rightarrow \text{Follicle growth} \rightarrow \text{Estrogen} \uparrow \rightarrow \text{LH surge} \rightarrow \text{Ovulation}$$

$$\text{Corpus Luteum} \rightarrow \text{Progesterone} \uparrow \rightarrow \text{Endometrium maintained}$$

$$\text{No fertilization} \rightarrow \text{Corpus Luteum degenerates} \rightarrow \text{Progesterone} \downarrow \rightarrow \text{Menstruation}$$
