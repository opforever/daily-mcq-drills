# 8.1 Agriculture as a System and Types of Farming


Pakistan is an agricultural country, and agriculture is the main source of food production for the country's ever-increasing population. Agriculture is the cultivation of land for the production of crops and the raising of animals. It is a primary industry, meaning that it extracts raw materials from the earth. Agricultural raw materials can be processed in industry or consumed directly.

## How Agriculture Works as a System

Agriculture can be viewed as a system of inputs, processes, and outputs.

### i. Inputs

Inputs can be divided into two main categories. **Natural (Physical)** inputs include land and other abiotic elements such as soil, water, and climate, which are essential for agriculture as they are used to grow crops and raise livestock. **Human (Economic)** inputs include skilled and semi-skilled labour, machinery, investment, seeds, fertilizers, and pesticides, which are used to improve the productivity and efficiency of agricultural production.

### ii. Processes

The inputs regulate the type of processes required on the farm. Processes are the activities used to convert inputs into outputs, divided into two main categories: **crop production** (planting, weeding, irrigating, and harvesting) and **livestock production** (feeding, breeding, and caring for animals).

### iii. Outputs

Outputs are the products produced by the agricultural system. The outputs of livestock production (livestock and dairy products) can be used as inputs to the food system or to other industries, such as the leather industry. Outputs can be divided into food products, such as wheat, rice, milk, meat, and eggs, and non-food products, such as cotton, wool, and biofuels. Profit is typically reinvested back into the farm for new investment, equipment, land, or livestock.

## Types of Farming in Pakistan

There are three main types of farming practiced in Pakistan: small-scale subsistence farming, cash crop farming, and livestock farming.

### A. Small-Scale Subsistence Farming

Pakistan is a land of subsistence farmers. Subsistence farming is based on growing crops and rearing animals mainly for the farmer's own use; if there is a surplus, it is sold for profit. 90% of farmers in Pakistan are small-scale subsistence farmers, meaning they own less than 12.5 acres of land.

<CaptionedImage src="kg24kc9ngmjdg5d7vsnb7rp8td8bv4f3" alt="Small-scale subsistence farm in Pakistan" caption="Subsistence farming in Pakistan" />

Subsistence farmers account for 42% of the country's workforce and contribute about 19% to the country's GDP. They produce about 60% of Pakistan's agricultural output, playing a vital role in the country's food security, and produce most of the country's basic food crops, such as wheat, rice, and maize.

Subsistence farmers in Pakistan use traditional methods of cultivation, relying on manual labor for sowing, weeding, harvesting, and threshing. Animals are used instead of machines to pull wooden ploughs and carts and to help with threshing. Small-scale subsistence farming in Pakistan is a labor-intensive activity, so women and children are also involved in farming processes, including caring for the animals. They prepare feed by chopping fodder and mixing different types of feed, deliver feed to the animals, milk buffaloes, cows, and goats, collect eggs, raise chickens, clean animals, and help with harvest.

### B. Cash Crop Farming

Cash crop farming is commercial farming in which the farm output, including crops and animals, is sold to make a profit. Cash crops are cultivated primarily for sale rather than for consumption by the farmer or their family, are typically grown on a large scale, and are often exported. Examples include cotton, sugarcane, wheat, rice, corn, tobacco, oilseeds, fruits, and vegetables.

Cash crop farms typically employ a mix of skilled and unskilled labor. Skilled labor is often needed for operating machinery, applying pesticides and herbicides, and maintaining the farm's infrastructure, while unskilled labor is often used for tasks such as planting, weeding, and harvesting.

Pakistan has two cropping seasons: Kharif (summer, April to October) and Rabi (winter, October to April). Winters in the lowlands are warm enough for temperate crops to grow, such as wheat and rapeseed, which are summer crops in the UK and other northwestern European countries. Pakistan's summer crops, such as cotton and rice, require high temperatures, like those in the tropics.

Water supply is a big problem for cash crop farming in Pakistan. Farmers can only grow crops in both seasons if they have enough irrigation water. Numerous dams (large and small) and barrages have been built to divert and store water from rivers that originate in the Himalayas. The two largest storage dams are Tarbela on the Indus and Mangla on the Jhelum. These dams supply water to large link canals, which in turn feed water into many smaller canals that crisscross the Punjab and Sindh plains. Large barrages across the Indus in the south, such as Sukkur, are supplied with water from the Tarbela Dam in winter when river flow is low. Tube wells are also used to pump water from the ground for irrigation, especially in areas where canal irrigation is not available or adequate.

# 8.2 Major Food Crops: Wheat, Rice and Maize


## Wheat

Wheat is Pakistan's main staple crop and, therefore, essential for the food security of the country. It accounts for 9.2% of the value added in agriculture and 1.8% of the GDP. Self-sufficiency in wheat has been a core objective of every government. Wheat crop recorded historic high production of 27.293 million tonnes, an increase of 8.1 percent over the 25.248 million tonnes produced in 2020, primarily due to an increase in cultivated area. Significant high-yielding wheat varieties include Maxi Pak and MH-21. By using high-yielding wheat varieties, farmers can increase their yields and profits.

**Geographical Requirements:** Wheat is a relatively adaptable crop and can be grown in a variety of geographical conditions. It prefers well-drained, loamy soils with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. It requires moderate rainfall, though it can be grown in areas with less rainfall if irrigation is available. Wheat is a cool-season crop and requires temperatures between 10°C and 20°C for optimal growth; temperatures above 30°C can stress the crop and reduce yields. Wheat can be grown in rainfed conditions, but irrigation is often necessary to achieve high yields, especially in areas with low rainfall or during droughts.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2btyww8f96p7zaf6zxkrdbbs8bt1dg" alt="Wheat field in Pakistan" caption="Wheat crop" />

**Wheat Production Areas:** During 2020-21, the area under cultivation increased by 4.2 percent to 9,178 thousand hectares over the previous year's sown area of 8,805 thousand hectares. Punjab is the largest wheat-producing province in Pakistan, accounting for over 70% of the country's total wheat production. Other major wheat-producing provinces include Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan. Major wheat-growing areas include Faisalabad, Toba Tek Singh, Jhang, and Chakwal in Punjab; Jacobabad, Larkana, and Shikarpur in Sindh; Mardan, Nowshera, and Swabi in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; and Quetta, Pishin, and Zhob in Balochistan.

## Rice

Rice is a major food and cash crop in Pakistan. It is the second most important staple food crop after wheat and the second most important export commodity after cotton. Rice contributes 3.5% of value added in agriculture and 0.7% of GDP. Rice production is divided into two categories: Basmati (fine) and Coarse. The production of coarse types has been increasing in recent years. In 2020-21, rice was cultivated on 3,335 thousand hectares, 9.9% more than the previous year.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2e1fjya7n6ktg5dx95sngbvd8bt5ch" alt="Rice field in Pakistan" caption="Rice crop" />

**Geographical Requirements:** Rice requires a temperature range of 20°C to 35°C for optimal growth; temperatures below 20°C can stunt plant growth, and temperatures above 40°C can reduce yields. It requires moderate to high rainfall, ranging from 1270 mm to 2000 mm, though it can be grown in areas with less rainfall if irrigation is available. Rice prefers well-drained, loamy soils with a pH of 6.5 to 7.5.

**Method of Cultivation:** The soil is tilled and levelled to prepare the seedbed. Rice seeds are sown in the seedbed, and seedlings are transplanted to the main field when they are 3-4 weeks old. Rice requires regular irrigation, especially during the first few months after transplanting, and a balanced fertilizer should be applied every 4-6 weeks. It is also important to keep the rice field free of weeds, as they compete with the rice for water and nutrients and can harbour pests and diseases. Rice is typically harvested 3-4 months after transplanting, by hand or by machine, and the harvested rice is then threshed to remove the grains from the stalks.

**Areas of Cultivation:** Punjab is the largest rice-producing province, accounting for over 70% of the country's total rice production, with major growing districts including Faisalabad, Sheikhupura, Gujranwala, Hafizabad, Sialkot, and Narowal. Sindh accounts for around 20% of production, with major districts including Jacobabad, Larkana, and Shikarpur. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa accounts for around 10%, with major districts including Mardan, Nowshera, and Swabi. In Balochistan, a very small amount of rice is cultivated.

**Problems of the Rice Crop:** Rice is susceptible to a number of pests and diseases, such as rice stem borers, brown plant hoppers, and blast disease, as well as abiotic stresses such as drought, salinity, and flooding. Weeds compete with rice for water and nutrients and can also harbour pests and diseases. Rice is a heavy feeder and requires regular fertilization; nutrient deficiencies can lead to reduced yields and poor grain quality.

**Management Strategies:** Pests and diseases can be managed through a combination of cultural practices such as crop rotation, biological control using natural predators, and chemical pesticides as a last resort. Laser leveling, a land preparation technique using lasers to create a level surface, can improve water distribution and reduce water waste. Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) is a water management practice in which rice fields are alternately flooded and drained, reducing water consumption without sacrificing yield. Nutrient deficiencies can be managed through regular, balanced fertilization every 4-6 weeks.

## Maize

Maize is the third most important cereal crop of Pakistan after wheat and rice. It contributes 3.4 percent to the value added in agriculture and 0.6 percent to GDP. Maize is cultivated as a multipurpose crop for food, particularly in the manufacturing of corn flour and custard powder, as well as feed and fodder for animals and poultry. While human consumption is declining, its utilization in the feed and wet milling industry is growing at a fast pace.

<CaptionedImage src="kg252135dkg3rpybqaepdepxq18bt8b9" alt="Maize field in Pakistan" caption="Maize crop" />

During 2020-21, maize was cultivated on an area of 1,418 thousand hectares, an increase of 1.0 percent over the previous year's 1,404 thousand hectares. This production increase was largely due to an increase in area, availability of improved seed varieties, and better economic returns. Its geographical requirements include a temperature of around 35°C with rainfall between 50-500 mm, and it can be grown effectively on porous soil.

# 8.3 Cash Crops and Fruit Farming


## Cotton

Cotton is a vital crop for both the agricultural and textile sectors of the economy. It accounts for around 0.6 percent of GDP and 3.1 percent of the value added in agriculture. Cotton is an export-oriented raw material for the textile industry and plays a significant role in Pakistan's foreign exchange earnings.

**Geographical Requirements:** Cotton requires moderate rainfall, though it can be grown in areas with less rainfall if irrigation is available. It is a warm-season crop requiring temperatures between 25°C and 35°C for optimal growth; temperatures below 20°C can stunt plant growth, and temperatures above 40°C can reduce yields. Cotton is a drought-tolerant crop, but irrigation is often necessary to achieve high yields, especially in areas with low rainfall or during droughts. It prefers well-drained, loamy soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5.

**Problems of the Cotton Crop:** Pakistan, a major cotton producer, is experiencing a significant decline in production due to multiple challenges: cotton is no longer as profitable as other crops such as maize, paddy, and sugarcane (economic); whitefly and pink bollworm infestations, along with leaf curl virus, are damaging crops (biotic stresses); climate change, heat stress, and extreme rainfall are also impacting production (abiotic stresses); unsustainable farming practices such as poor soil management and excessive pesticide use are contributing to the decline; fertilizers, pesticides, and seeds are expensive, making it difficult for farmers to turn a profit; and there is a shortage of early-generation seeds and inadequate research on new varieties that can withstand pests, diseases, and climatic stresses.

**Revival of Cotton Production:** To revive cotton production, essential measures include setting a minimum indicative or intervention price so farmers receive a fair price even when market prices are low; issuing subsidies to farmers in a timely manner to reduce input costs; improving extension services to educate farmers about best practices, including the use of high-yielding varieties (HYVs) and awareness of irrigation and soil management needs; developing and distributing genetically modified (GM) cotton seeds resistant to pests and diseases; and issuing subsidies for pesticides, seeds, and fertilizers through Kissan cards, along with loans on easy terms and conditions.

## Sugarcane

Sugarcane is a valuable cash crop in Pakistan, accounting for 3.4% of agriculture's value addition and 0.7% of GDP. In 2020-21, the area under sugarcane cultivation increased by 12% to 1,165 thousand hectares, and production increased by 22% to 81.009 million tonnes, due to favorable weather conditions, better management, timely availability of inputs, and higher economic returns.

**Geographical Requirements:** Sugarcane requires a temperature range of 20°C to 30°C for optimal growth; temperatures below 20°C can stunt plant growth, and temperatures above 40°C can reduce yields. It requires moderate rainfall, ranging from 100 to 1520 mm, though it can be grown in areas with less rainfall if irrigation is available. It prefers well-drained, loamy soils with a pH of 6.5 to 7.5; clay soils can be too heavy and poorly draining, while sandy soils may not retain enough moisture.

**Method of Cultivation:** The soil is prepared by tilling it to a depth of at least 30 cm, and organic matter is added to improve drainage and fertility. Sugarcane stalk is cut to 30-45 cm lengths and planted in furrows 10-15 cm deep and 75-100 cm apart. Sugarcane requires regular irrigation and regular fertilization, especially potash, and the field must be kept free of weeds, which compete for water and nutrients.

<CaptionedImage src="kg29jz7r8jsq0gp7754afvxryd8bvv26" alt="Sugarcane field in Pakistan" caption="Sugarcane crop" />

**Growing Regions:** Punjab is the largest sugarcane-producing province, accounting for over 60% of total production, with major districts including Faisalabad, Toba Tek Singh, and Jhang. Sindh is second, accounting for around 25%, with major districts including Jacobabad, Larkana, and Shikarpur. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is third, accounting for around 10%, with major districts including Mardan, Nowshera, and Swabi.

## Tobacco and Other Crops

Tobacco is grown in Pakistan on around 0.25 percent of irrigated land. The major tobacco-growing areas are in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, specifically in Swabi, Mardan, Nowshera, and Charsadda, which produce over 90% of the country's Flue Cured Virginia (FCV) tobacco. Tobacco is grown either by transplanting seedlings from seedbeds or by direct sowing in the main field. It is a labour-intensive crop requiring regular attention: farmers weed the fields, water the plants, apply fertilizer, harvest the leaves at the right time, and cure them properly.

</SideActivity>

<CaptionedImage src="kg204683k0jjqn401gn6ccs9y18bvvyc" alt="Tobacco field in Pakistan" caption="Tobacco field" />

Gram, Bajra, and Jowar production decreased in 2020-21 due to smaller areas under cultivation and unfavorable weather conditions. Barley, Rapeseed and Mustard, and Tobacco production remained at low levels, similar to the previous year. Production of pulses including Moong, Mash, and Potato increased significantly, while production of Chillies and Onion decreased slightly, and Masoor production remained the same. Pakistan imported 2.917 million tonnes of edible oil in 2021, while local production was only 0.374 million tonnes.

## Fruit Farming

Fruit farming is a significant part of Pakistan's economy, providing employment and generating foreign exchange earnings. Pakistan is the fifth largest producer of fruits in the world, producing a wide variety including apples, mangoes, citrus fruits, bananas, grapes, guavas, dates, and peaches.

<CaptionedImage src="kg24663ngx98nqkp00d2jperx58bv4ce" alt="Fruit farming in Pakistan" caption="Fruit Farming in Pakistan" />

Fruit farming is practiced in all four provinces, but the major growing regions are Punjab, Sindh, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Punjab is the largest fruit-producing province, accounting for over 60% of total production, with major districts including Multan, Faisalabad, Toba Tek Singh, and Sargodha. Sindh accounts for around 25%, with major districts including Jacobabad, Larkana, and Shikarpur. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa accounts for around 10%, with major districts including Mardan, Nowshera, and Swabi. Hunza and Swat are known for apples and apricots. In Balochistan, varieties of apples, dates, apricots, and grapes are produced. Pakistan is also a major producer of dry fruits, such as almonds, walnuts, and raisins.

**Government Support Schemes for Fruit Farmers:** The government provides subsidies to farmers for purchasing fruit plants and fertilizers, along with training on modern fruit farming practices. The National Program for the Development of Horticulture (NPDH) provides subsidies for fruit plants and other agricultural inputs, and training on practices such as cold storage for protecting fruit and preventing harvest loss, pest and disease management, irrigation, fertilization, and harvesting.

## Contribution of Agriculture to the Employment Sector

Agriculture is essential to Pakistan's economy, food security, and employment. It contributes 19.2% to GDP and employs 38.5% of the labor force. Over 65% of the population relies on agriculture for livelihood. However, agricultural growth has been slow due to shrinking arable land, climate change, water shortages, and rural-to-urban migration. Increasing agricultural productivity requires new approaches. Agriculture can play a vital role in spurring economic growth, but it faces challenges such as climate change, water shortage, and rising input prices.

<SideActivity title="Do You Know?">
Sustainable approaches to agricultural development include a number of organic farming methods: crop rotation (planting different crops in sequence in the same field to improve soil fertility and reduce pests and diseases, with one patch left fallow); intercropping (planting two or more crops in the same field at the same time to improve soil fertility and reduce weed competition); cover cropping (planting crops that cover the soil surface to prevent erosion); composting (making compost from plant residues, manure, and food scraps); and biological pest control (using natural predators and parasites, such as ladybugs to control aphids and lacewings to control whiteflies). Farmland must not be converted into housing schemes, and more water schemes may help sustain agricultural activities. Organic farming can be practiced on scales ranging from small backyard gardens to large commercial farms; in Pakistan it is most commonly practiced by small-scale farmers.
</SideActivity>

# 8.4 Livestock Farming


Livestock farming is the raising and management of domesticated animals for the production of meat, milk, eggs, and other animal products. It is one of the oldest and most important forms of agriculture and a significant contributor to the country's economy, accounting for around 11% of GDP. The sector also plays an important role in food security, providing a source of high-quality protein. Livestock farming can be divided into three main types: nomadic, settled, and transhumance.

## Nomadic

Nomadic livestock farming is a traditional form of livestock farming in which herders move their animals from place to place in search of pasture and water. It is typically practiced in arid and semi-arid regions where resources are limited, by the Baloch and Pashtun tribes in the Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provinces. The main types of livestock raised by nomadic farmers are sheep and goats.

<CaptionedImage src="kg258gnacgx2j2hvyhhs1hssnn8bv3cc" alt="Nomadic livestock herders with sheep and goats" caption="Nomadic Livestock" />

## Settled

Settled livestock farming is a type of livestock farming in which animals are kept in permanent enclosures. It is typically practiced in areas with more abundant resources, such as arable land and water, and is practiced throughout Pakistan but is particularly concentrated in Punjab and Sindh. The main types of livestock raised by settled farmers are cattle, buffaloes, goats, and poultry.

## Transhumance

Transhumance livestock farming is a type of livestock farming in which herders move their animals between different pastures seasonally. It is typically practiced in mountainous regions where there is seasonal variation in the availability of pasture, by the Gujjar and Bakarwal tribes in the Kashmir region. The main types of livestock raised by transhumance farmers are sheep and goats.

# 8.5 Marine and Inland Fisheries


The fisheries subsector of agriculture in Pakistan is concerned with the harvesting and processing of aquatic resources, such as fish, shellfish, and crustaceans. It is a major contributor to the country's economy, providing food security, employment, and export revenue. Fisheries accounted for 1.6% of total exports in 2020. The major importing countries for Pakistani fisheries are Thailand, China, Vietnam, the UAE, South Korea, Malaysia, Kuwait, Japan, Saudi Arabia, and Indonesia. During the Covid pandemic, fish exports declined significantly, from 22 million in 2017 to 6 million in 2020.

## Fish Production in Pakistan

Fisheries are divided into three main types: inland fisheries, marine fisheries, and aquaculture. Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KPK) only have inland fisheries, while Balochistan has marine fisheries and Sindh has both. Fish production data for Punjab includes production from Mangla Dam, and data for KPK includes production from Gilgit-Baltistan.

## Marine Fishing

Pakistan's 1,050-kilometer coastline along the Arabian Sea is home to two major fishing centers: Sindh and Makran. These coasts have rich marine fishing grounds that contribute about 64% of the country's total fish production, compared to 36% from inland fisheries. Karachi and Korangi are the main fishing ports of Sindh, while Makran has smaller ports such as Sonmiani, Jiwani, and Ormara. Gwadar port is also being developed as a modern fishing center with a state-of-the-art fish curing yard, ice factory, and refrigeration plant.

### Types of Marine Fishing

**Subsistence fishing** is fishing for the purpose of consumption by the fisherman and his family, as fish is the main component of their diet. It is carried out by small-scale fishermen using traditional methods such as handlines, nets, and traps. Traditional boats can stay only up to 5 km from the coast. Since the catch is small, subsistence fishermen generate alternative income by making fish feed, repairing fish nets and boats, and similar activities.

**Commercial fishing** is fishing for the purpose of selling the catch and making a profit. It is carried out by larger-scale fishing vessels using more modern methods such as trawling, purse seining, and gill netting. Mechanized boats can stay 50-60 km offshore in deep water for large catches. Commercial fishing is a major economic activity in Pakistan, generating export revenue and providing employment to thousands of people.

### Types of Marine Fish

Sharks, Drums, Mackerel, Croakers, Demersal fish, and Catfish.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2dbj0yez0k9nxyscpbfs710x8bvrwf" alt="White Pomfret, a marine fish species" caption="White Pomfret (marine species)" />

### Sustainable Development of Marine Fisheries

**Set catch limits:** The government can set catch limits for different fish species based on scientific assessments of sustainable yields, helping to prevent overfishing and giving fish populations a chance to recover.

**Close fishing grounds during spawning seasons:** Spawning seasons are critical for fish reproduction. Closing fishing grounds during these periods helps protect fish stocks and ensures future generations of fish can thrive.

**Ban destructive fishing equipment:** Destructive fishing equipment such as dynamite and bottom trawls can damage fish habitats and kill fish indiscriminately. Banning such gear protects fish stocks and the marine ecosystem.

<CaptionedImage src="kg20253w8xv79frjjv2av06a5h8bvf8t" alt="Small-scale subsistence fishing boat" caption="Subsistence fishing" />

## Inland Fisheries

Inland fisheries in Pakistan employ a variety of fishing methods, including cast netting, gill netting, trawling, hook and line fishing, and spearfishing, to harvest fish and shellfish from freshwater lakes, rivers, reservoirs, and abandoned channels of canals and rivers. The most common fish species caught in inland fisheries are carp, catfish, perch, Palla, Thalla, Rahu, and Trout.

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), inland fisheries in Pakistan employed over 211,609 people in 2014, representing a significant portion of total employment in the Pakistani fisheries sector. Inland fishing is an important source of employment in rural areas, providing a livelihood for many people who may not have other job opportunities, and plays a role in reducing poverty and improving food security.

### Sustainable Development of Inland Fisheries

The Pakistani government is working to promote the sustainable development of inland fisheries through measures such as stocking fish populations, regulating fishing practices, and protecting fish habitats.

# 8.6 Aquaculture and the Fishing Industry


## Aquaculture / Fish Farming

Aquaculture is the farming of fish and shellfish in controlled environments. Aquaculture fisheries are becoming increasingly important as the demand for seafood grows. Fish farms need a reliable supply of clean water, used to fill the ponds and maintain water quality. Fish feed is purchased from commercial suppliers or produced on-farm. Fish farms typically use equipment such as pumps, aerators, and feeders to maintain water quality, feed the fish, and monitor their growth and health. Trees are planted to maintain air quality and oxygen levels. The main fishing centres are Haleji in Thatta, Manchar Lake in Dadu district, the river Indus at Kotri and Sukkur, and the reservoirs of the Tarbela and Mangla dams.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2320rp9d0dgn1qn3hqh898jd8bth0v" alt="Trout fish farm" caption="Trout Fish Farm" />

**Types of Farm Fish:** Almost all inland types of fish are reared at farms, including Palla, Thalla, Rahu, Murrel, Grass carp, Silver carp, and Trout.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2accqzseqqwh2dgezgc5y1j58bvfp2" alt="Types of farmed fish" caption="Types of farm fish" />

## Problems of the Fishing Industry

There are four major problems facing the fish industry in Pakistan.

**Overfishing:** The biggest threat to the sustainable development of the fish industry, caused by high demand for fish, inadequate fishing regulations, and illegal fishing.

**Pollution:** Water pollution from industrial and agricultural waste contaminates fish stocks and makes them unsafe for human consumption.

**Climate change:** Climate change is causing sea levels to rise and water temperatures to increase, disrupting fish habitats and making it more difficult for fish to reproduce.

**Lack of infrastructure and technology:** The fish industry lacks modern infrastructure and technology, making it difficult for fishermen to catch and process fish efficiently.

## Government Efforts to Develop Fisheries

**Curtailing overfishing:** The government is working to reduce overfishing through measures including setting catch limits, closing fishing grounds during spawning seasons, and banning destructive fishing gear.

**Fighting pollution:** The government is working to combat water pollution by investing in wastewater treatment plants, regulating industrial and agricultural runoff, and raising awareness of the importance of clean water.

**Adapting to climate change:** The government is investing in coastal infrastructure, developing new fishing methods, and helping fishermen diversify their livelihoods.

**Improving infrastructure and technology:** The government is investing in fishing harbors, processing facilities, and cold storage facilities, and providing training and support to fishermen on modern fishing methods and technologies.

## What I Have Learned

- The agriculture sector is facing problems of water scarcity, climate change, and land degradation.
- Agriculture provides food for a growing population and helps to lift people out of poverty.
- Kharif crops are grown during the monsoon season, i.e., June to September, in Pakistan.
- Rabi crops are sown in October and November and harvested in February and March. They provide food during winters.
- Agriculture as a system is a complex web of inputs, processes, and outputs that work together to produce food and other agricultural products.
- Pakistan has a long coastline and a number of inland water bodies, which provide abundant fishing resources. The fishing sector employs over 2 million people in Pakistan and contributes about 1% to the country's GDP.
