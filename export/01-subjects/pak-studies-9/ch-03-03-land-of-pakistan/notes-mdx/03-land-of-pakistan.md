# 3.1 Location of Pakistan


Pakistan is located in South Asia with a land area of 796,096 km². It is 1,600 km long from north to south and 885 km wide from east to west. According to the coordinates shown on the map, Pakistan is located between the latitudes of 24°N and 37°N and extends from longitude 61°E to 77°E. Islamabad is the federal capital of Pakistan, and one of the most modern and well-planned capitals in the world.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2b3kv1w0eg1443kggvxjwgwd8btnsa" alt="A map of Pakistan showing its four provinces and administrative regions" caption="Administrative divisions of Pakistan" />

Pakistan has four provinces, Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan, and Sindh. Pakistan's Northern Areas were assigned a new identity, Gilgit-Baltistan, with internal and political autonomy granted by the federal cabinet of Pakistan on 29 August 2009. Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), a self-governing territory with its capital at Muzaffarabad, is also administered by the federal government of Pakistan. In the south, Pakistan has a 1,058 km long coastal area.

Islamabad is known for its profuse green spaces, including the Margalla Hills National Park and the Shakarparian. It is also the site of several renowned landmarks, including the country's emblematic Faisal Mosque, a stunning example of modern Islamic architecture.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2ccxqk5jp5rc9jfnyfz0pmph8btxan" alt="A photograph of the Faisal Mosque in Islamabad" caption="Faisal Mosque, Islamabad" />

Pakistan is bordered by six countries. To the east, Pakistan shares its international border with India. At the northern end of Pakistan, the Line of Control (LOC, 806 km) separates Pakistani-administered Kashmir from Indian-held Kashmir territory. China lies to the northeast, across the remote and mountainous Karakoram range. The western border with Afghanistan is established along the 2,611 km long Durand Line. The Wakhan Corridor, a narrow strip of Afghan territory jutting into Tajikistan, separates Pakistan from the rest of Central Asia. In the southwest, Pakistan shares a 909 km long border with the Islamic Republic of Iran.

## Significance of Pakistan's Location

Strategically, Pakistan's location makes it a potential gateway to Central Asia and the Middle East. It also serves as a doorway to the Islamic bloc, giving Pakistan a strategic advantage in the region for both cooperation and competition with other countries:

i. Pakistan is situated to the northwest of China and to the northeast of Iran and Afghanistan, and is also close to Central Asia. Pakistan provides a transit trade route to Afghanistan and the Central Asian states, which are landlocked countries with no access to the sea. This gives Pakistan access to Central Asia, a region with rich natural resources and growing economies.

ii. The Arabian Sea is a valuable resource for many countries from the viewpoint of trade by sea. It is a warm-water sea and provides a major shipping lane and a source of oil and gas, connecting the Indian Ocean to the Persian Gulf.

A landlocked country is surrounded by land, with no direct access to the sea or coastline. Examples of such countries include Afghanistan and Bhutan.

This makes the Arabian Sea a major shipping route for sea trade between South Asia, the Far East, and the Middle East, giving Pakistan access to global markets and energy resources.

iii. Pakistan is located at the intersection of the world's two busiest air traffic corridors, the North Atlantic and Asia-Pacific routes, making it a natural transit hub for air traffic between East and West. The Jinnah Terminal, Karachi is important as an international airport because it handles large passenger and cargo traffic.

iv. The Arabian Sea serves as an important source of seafood, providing habitats for many marine creatures including fish, sharks, whales, and dolphins. Pakistan has three important seaports: Karachi Keamari port, Qasim port, and Gwadar port.

<SideActivity title="Expand Your Horizon">
The China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) is a 62-billion-dollar infrastructure project that connects China to the Arabian Sea. CPEC is a crucial part of China's Belt and Road Initiative, an investment project that aims to connect China to countries around the world largely through roads and railways. CPEC is expected to boost Pakistan's economy and improve its trade links with China and other countries of the world.
</SideActivity>

# 3.2 Major Cities of Pakistan


Major cities are often the economic centers of their countries. They are home to businesses, industries, and financial institutions that play a vital role in the economy. Some cities have important historical and cultural sites, such as museums, monuments, and theaters. By learning about the history and culture of these cities, we can better understand our own country's past and present, and develop an appreciation for the diversity of our country and its people.

## Karachi

Karachi, Pakistan's financial hub and the capital of Sindh province, is considered an important city due to its strategic location and economic potential. As a pioneer in cable networking and information technology, Karachi has become a center of software outsourcing. It is home to a number of leading industries, such as textile manufacturing, shipping, and tourism. Karachi is also a major transportation hub, with the country's largest airport and seaport, facilitating exports and imports.

<CaptionedImage src="kg20g7ed61e224fbh58vnaf8898bvbx8" alt="A photograph of Karachi city" caption="Karachi" />

## Lahore

Lahore is the second-most populous metropolis in Pakistan, situated in Punjab province. It is a leading cultural and historical metropolis, home to the Badshahi Mosque, one of the world's largest mosques. Lahore is also a major commercial and industrial center for manufacturing, particularly in the textile, food processing, and engineering industries.

<CaptionedImage src="kg29a96kcd93tw7ks9rkngpjqd8btmqy" alt="A photograph of Lahore city" caption="Lahore" />

## Faisalabad

Faisalabad is situated in the north-east of the country, lying between the plains of the Ravi and Chenab Rivers. It is one of the most industrialized cities in Pakistan and known as the "Cottonopolis of Pakistan" owing to its textile industry. Agriculturally, Faisalabad produces cotton, wheat, rice, and sugarcane as its main crops.

<CaptionedImage src="kg23g0ztfg75m5bfczsxvgb62d8bvery" alt="A photograph of Faisalabad city" caption="Faisalabad" />

<CaptionedImage src="kg2c185wwd94ayb564wnsgfvvd8btzjz" alt="A photograph of the Kohinoor shopping district in Faisalabad" caption="Kohinoor shopping district, Faisalabad" />

## Multan

Multan is the fourth-largest city in Pakistan, situated in Punjab province. It is an ancient metropolis with a storied history, home to the Multan Fort, one of the country's oldest forts. Multan is also a leading religious city, home to many shrines of Sufi saints, including Hazrat Shah Rukn-e-Alam.

<CaptionedImage src="kg27cw4cpxzpn42e7bams7wcpx8bvphq" alt="A photograph of Multan city" caption="Multan" />

## Peshawar

Peshawar is the metropolis of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, situated in the northwest of Pakistan. It is a leading transportation hub, for example, the Khyber Pass is a historic trade route between Central Asia and South Asia. Peshawar is also a leading cultural city and is the center of a number of historical monuments, such as the Mohabbat Khan Mosque.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2d0gqg3h5azcnyf4qfkfrev18btyfx" alt="A photograph of Peshawar city" caption="Peshawar" />

## Quetta

Quetta is the capital of Balochistan province, situated in the south-west of Pakistan. It is renowned for its orchards producing plums, peaches, pomegranates, apricots, apples, melons, cherries, pistachios, and almonds. Quetta has mineral wealth such as coal, copper, and gold. It is also a hub of education, business, and commerce, and is known for its handicrafts.

<CaptionedImage src="kg212f1pm2d63se2mza311cm798btw13" alt="A photograph of Quetta city" caption="Quetta" />

## Gilgit-Baltistan

Gilgit-Baltistan is a region located in the northern mountains. It is a popular tourist destination, home to a number of glaciers, including the Baltoro, Siachen, and Batura glaciers. Gilgit-Baltistan is also a major source of hydroelectric power for Pakistan. CPEC, a massive economic project under the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), aims to enhance the socioeconomic and political dividends in Gilgit-Baltistan. It is projected to generate more than half a million jobs by establishing Special Economic Zones in Gilgit, invigorating the tourism industry, and expanding the reach of e-commerce. CPEC-driven hydropower development, technical expertise, and human resource development have transformed the region's socioeconomic dynamics.

<CaptionedImage src="kg288afcfx34avh5qc6y1wvpds8btrp7" alt="A scenic photograph of the Skardu region" caption="A scenic view of Skardu" />

## Rawalpindi

Rawalpindi is located in Punjab province and serves as a major cantonment area. It is a major transportation hub, with a network of roads, railways, and airports connecting it to other parts of Pakistan, making it easy for businesses to import and export goods and services. There are several important commercial and wholesale markets in Rawalpindi that sell goods in bulk to retailers, and its manufacturing and construction industries are significant.

## What I Have Learned

- Pakistan is situated on important commercial corridors that connect South Asia, Central Asia, and the Middle East, making it a strategically significant site for trade and commerce.
- The Arabian Sea is a source of maritime trade.
- Pakistan provides a transit route to landlocked countries.
- CPEC is a project of China that is lucrative for the economic growth of Pakistan.
- Lahore is the second-largest city of Pakistan after Karachi. It is an industrial hub of Pakistan.
- Faisalabad is known for producing world-class textile goods.
- Pakistan and India have very complex relations.
- On August 29, 2009, the federal cabinet of Pakistan granted internal and political autonomy to Gilgit-Baltistan.
