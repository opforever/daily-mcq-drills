# 4.1 Northern and North Western Mountains


Topography is the study of the earth's surface and the representation of both natural and artificial features of land on a map. The study of topography is fundamental to understanding the Earth's surface features and how they have changed over time. The natural topography of Pakistan can be classified into six main regions:

1. The Northern Mountains and North Western Mountains
2. The Western Mountains
3. The Balochistan Plateau
4. Pothwar Plateau and Salt Range
5. The Indus Plain
6. Desert Areas

<CaptionedImage src="kg24eqk1dqz40202fg43cn23xs8bv9t2" alt="Map of Pakistan's natural topographical regions" caption="Regions of Pakistan" />

## Northern Mountains and North Western Mountains

This region includes the following three mountain ranges.

### i. Himalayas (Altitude about 4000 m)

The Himalayas cover 2500 km from east to west. They include the Siwaliks, Lesser Himalayas, and the Central Himalayas. The Siwalik hills have an altitude between 600-1200 meters, while the Lesser Himalayas have an altitude between 1800-4500 meters. The Himalayas have tremendous tourist potential, with places such as Murree and the Galiats, including Ghora Gali and Nathia Gali, situated there. The Central Himalayas lie between the Pir Punjal and Karakoram ranges. Most of the Central Himalayas are located in Kashmir, with the highest peak, Nanga Parbat, having a height of about 8126 meters. The natural topography is characterized by snow-peaked mountains and massive glaciers with rich natural vegetation, including tropical evergreen coniferous forests, deciduous forests, and alpine meadows. Above the tree line, vegetation is in the form of grasses, shrubs, and wild flowers.

<CaptionedImage src="kg22tjxkzg2ra80wc1nz1pjt1d8bv57b" alt="Sparsely populated Himalayan landscape" caption="Sparsely Populated Himalayas" />

### ii. Hindukush (Altitude about 5000 m)

The Hindukush range stretches from central and eastern Afghanistan into northwestern Pakistan and far southeastern Tajikistan. It is the western part of the Hindu Kush Himalayan Region (HKH) and is considered part of the greater Himalayan mountain system. These mountains have a rugged landscape due to steep-sided narrow valleys, e.g., Tirch Mir, which has an altitude of 7690 m. Due to the cold climate, natural vegetation is found in the form of alpine forests. Plants do not grow above the snow line, which starts above 4000 m. At this altitude, mountains remain covered with snow round the year. The glaciers, including Batura and Chiantar, are a vital source of water for the region and also support important economic activities such as agriculture and hydropower generation.

<CaptionedImage src="kg299h5sktts5ahw3cq831p4ed8bt412" alt="Hindukush mountain range" caption="Hindukush Mountains" />

The Hindu Kush mountains consist of a number of strategically important passes, including the Shandur Pass, the Shangla Pass, and the Lawarai Pass. These passes connect important population centers and valleys, such as Gilgit, Chitral, Swat, and Peshawar.

### iii. Karakoram Mountains (Average Altitude 6000 m)

The Karakoram range consists of a long chain of rocky mountains which are generally snow-capped, running east to west. The range stretches for 400 kilometers from the Hunza Valley to the Shyok River, with the westernmost part of the range in Pakistan. The Karakoram range is characterized by narrow and deep valleys, such as the Hunza, Shyok, and Baltoro; towering gorges, such as the Nanga Parbat and Indus gorges; and vast glaciers, such as the Baltoro, Siachen, and Biafo glaciers. K2, the second-highest mountain peak in the world, is also located in the Karakoram.

<CaptionedImage src="kg24dmekbwt7v7wnbh7dzgmghn8btgy4" alt="Karakoram mountain range" caption="Karakoram Mountains" />

The precipitation at lower elevations is typically in the form of rain. At higher elevations, precipitation is typically in the form of snow. Natural vegetation at lower elevations is mostly dry and steppe-like, with grasses, shrubs, and scattered trees such as poplar, juniper, and willow. At higher elevations, the vegetation becomes more alpine, with meadows, mosses, and lichens.

<CaptionedImage src="kg24aryms5hb0ex4y2afhgwfkn8btkqv" alt="High-altitude Karakoram landscape" caption="Karakoram high-altitude terrain" />

The population density in the Karakoram range is low due to the challenging climate and landscape. Most of the population is concentrated in Gilgit and Skardu in Gilgit-Baltistan. Most of the people who live in these high-altitude areas are subsistence farmers and pastoralists, growing crops and raising livestock for their own needs and trading surplus goods with other communities.

## Importance of Northern Mountains

i. The Northern Mountains are a major source of water for the region, providing drinking water, irrigation water, and hydropower. For example, the Indus River, which provides water for millions of people in Pakistan, originates in the Northern Mountains.

ii. The Northern Mountains play a role in regulating the climate of the region and the world. For example, the glaciers of the Northern Mountains help to cool the planet.

iii. The Northern Mountains are home to a variety of unique and endangered wildlife species, such as the snow leopard and the Tibetan antelope.

iv. Mountain peaks provide protection to Pakistan from cold winds of Siberia and Central Asia.

v. The Northern Mountains offer a variety of economic opportunities for local communities, such as tourism, agriculture, and forestry. For example, the Northern Mountains of Pakistan are a popular tourist destination for mountaineers and trekkers.

vi. The Northern Mountains provide raw materials to several important industries, e.g., chemical, pharmaceutical, pulp and paper, and furniture-making industries.

## Earthquake in Northern Areas

Northern Pakistan, including Kashmir, lies at the collision point of the Eurasian and Indian tectonic plates, making it prone to earthquakes. On October 8, 2005, a magnitude 7.6 earthquake struck the region, causing widespread devastation, especially in Balakot, Kashmir, which was almost completely destroyed. The earthquake resulted in a massive loss of life, damage to settlements and infrastructure, and large-scale migration to plain areas. Secondary effects included water shortages and the spread of diseases. The Pakistani government provided emergency relief, evacuation measures, and rehabilitation centers.

## Economic Activities and Lifestyle in the Western Mountains

The landscape of the western mountain ranges is rugged and largely devoid of natural vegetation. Most of the population lives in small towns and rural areas with a subsistence and nomadic lifestyle. Population density is lower than 100 people per square kilometer. Canal irrigation is not viable in the majority of the western mountains due to the challenging terrain. There is a lack of infrastructure facilities, including commuter trains, asphalted roadways, and air links; for instance, the large urban centers like Peshawar and Kohat are accessible through roads and railways and connected with the rest of the country. Although there are abundant mineral resources, they have not been extracted due to the tough terrain.

# 4.3 The Balochistan Plateau


The Balochistan Plateau forms the southwestern half of Pakistan. It is bordered to the north by the Hindu Kush mountains, to the south by the Arabian Sea, and to the west by the Iranian border. The plateau is characterized by its high-altitude ranges (600-3000 meters) with a total area stretching over 347,190 sq km. Balochistan has a dry climate, with less than 250 millimeters (10 inches) of annual rainfall on average.

The distinct topography of the Balochistan Plateau is identified as follows.

### i. Basins of North Balochistan

Between the Toba Kakar and the Suleman ranges, a number of depressions are found which are marked as the Zhob and Loralai basins. Quetta valley is located to the southwest of the Loralai basin.

<SideActivity title="Note for Teachers">
Inform students that a basin is a large, generally level region of land that is enclosed by higher land. Basins of Balochistan are formed by the erosion of the surrounding mountains and hills over time. They have no outlet to the sea.
</SideActivity>

### ii. Basins of Western Balochistan

The western basins are found between a number of mountain ranges such as the Chagai hills, Siahan, and central Makran. Balochistan has inland drainage. The rainwater of the basins drains into rivers and streams which are either absorbed into the ground or form temporary lakes called Hamuns, which contain high salt concentration. The largest Hamun is found in the Kharan desert.

### iii. Mountain Ranges

The mountain ranges found in the Balochistan Plateau are infertile and devoid of vegetation. However, these ranges contain vast deposits of coal, gold, copper, and sulphur. These ranges include the Central Brahui range, Toba Kakar range, Hala range, Makran coastal range, Siahan range, and Ras Koh range.

### iv. Coastal Areas

Coastal areas of the Balochistan Plateau are divided into eastern areas, including the Lasbela plain, and western areas consisting of the Makran coast, whose coastal plain is formed by the projection of land into the sea. There are some significant coastal rivers, i.e., Hab, Hingol, Porali, and Dasht, which empty their waters into the Arabian Sea.

## Natural and Economic Resources

The Balochistan Plateau is rich in natural resources, which are as follows.

**Water Resources:** Water resources in Balochistan are limited and unevenly distributed. People face water scarcity all round the year as there is no major source of water. The main sources of water are surface water and groundwater in the form of aquifers and springs. The system of Karez helps in irrigation. However, groundwater is declining due to over-extraction.

**Minerals:** Balochistan is rich in mineral wealth, which is possibly important but remains an under-developed sector in Balochistan's economy. According to the provincial Census report Balochistan 2017, the extraction of thirty-nine out of fifty recorded mineral resources present in the province generates an annual revenue close to Rs. 3.4 billion. The most important minerals are gas, coal, copper, antimony, and gold.

<SideActivity title="Expand Your Horizon">
Balochistan: A Trading Gateway

Gwadar port can be a hub for inter-regional transport and trade as it provides access to the Arabian Sea for landlocked Afghanistan and Central Asia. Gwadar has been marketed as a futuristic trade gateway with Central Asian regions. Goods from Gwadar port can be transported via land routes to Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, and deeper into Central Asia. To make this possible, road and railway networks are being laid linking Gwadar to Afghanistan and Iran. Plans to open gateways on the Afghanistan and Iran borders are also under execution. With these and similar developments, Balochistan can serve as a transit route into the neighboring countries of Afghanistan and Iran, and a trading gateway into Central Asia.
</SideActivity>

**Agriculture:**

*Fruit and vegetable production*, Balochistan has two crop seasons: Kharif (summer) and Rabi (winter). Kharif crops include rice, jowar, maize, onion, potato, melons, chilies, tobacco, and sugarcane. Kharif vegetables include lady fingers, tinda, brinjals, bitter gourd, bottle gourd, pumpkin, kharif tomatoes, and cucumber. Rabi crops include wheat, barley, rapeseed and mustard, cumin, gram, and sunflower. Rabi vegetables include cabbage, carrot, cauliflower, peas, radish, turnip, spinach, beet root, and Rabi tomatoes. Important fruits include almonds, apples, apricots, grapes, peaches, plums, pomegranates, cherries, dates, bananas, mangoes, chikoos, papayas, and coconuts.

*Fishing*, Balochistan's fishing sector has the potential to grow significantly, but it needs more support infrastructure and services. The important fish are Tuna, Sardines, and Mackerels. Its coastline is 70% of Pakistan's total coastline, but it only contributes 30% of the country's landed catch. This is due to a lack of supporting infrastructure and services at the ports of Gwadar, Ormara, and Pasni, as well as a lack of modern processing facilities. Integrating the development of the coastal highway and the Gwadar port is expected to boost the development of the fishing sector and the local economy.

*Livestock*, Balochistan is rich in livestock resources; almost 70 percent of the population is directly or indirectly involved in livestock rearing activities. Animals such as cattle, camels, and poultry are traditionally kept for household milk and meat needs. The main consumers of Balochistan's livestock products include the leather, carpet, and pharmaceutical industries. A poverty alleviation strategy with a focus on livestock development may well target the higher poverty in the province. By investing in infrastructure, services, and training, the government can help livestock farmers increase their productivity and incomes.

# 4.4 Pothwar Plateau and Salt Range


The Pothwar plateau and Salt range are located to the south of Islamabad, lying between the river Indus and river Jhelum. It has a changing landscape due to constant erosion. The Pothwar Plateau and Salt Range have rich deposits of minerals like gypsum, limestone, marble, dolomite, coal, and oil. A number of oil and gas fields are set up in this area, such as the Attock Oil Refinery. Farming depends on natural rainfall, which is why this area is called Barani (rainfed). Natural vegetation consists of dry temperate forest, with a mix of deciduous and evergreen trees and shrubs.

## Natural Topographical and Drainage Features of Pothwar

The Pothwar Plateau presents a badland topography owing to its dissected, eroded, and faulted land. The Soan river forms alluvial plains which are agriculturally productive.

**i. Ridges and residual hills**, These features are formed by the erosion of soft rock through the action of running water and fast-blowing winds. Kala Chitta and Khairi are important residual hills of the Pothwar plateau.

**ii. Faulted land**, During the mountain building process, the land is weak and shows off cracks.

**iii. Troughs and depressions**, These features are formed when soft rocks are eroded by water and wind blows away the loose soil, which creates depressions and troughs.

**iv. Ravines**, Ravines are deep channels created by the erosion of soft water and carry running water, particularly during the rainy season.

<CaptionedImage src="kg202nfpq7vcakvb65ren0kbxs8bv3b9" alt="Badland topography of Pothwar Plateau at Pharwala Fort along the Soan River" caption="Badland Topography of Pothwar Plateau (Pharwala Fort along the Soan River)" />

## The Salt Range

The Salt range is located 160 km from Islamabad. The Salt range consists of parallel ranges with an average altitude between 750 to 900 meters and is located in Khewra. The area is rich in mineral deposits such as gypsum, rock salt, and limestone. The Salt range consists of broken and faulted land eroded by rivers. It has the famous Kallar Kahar lake and Sakesar peak (1,527 m), which is the highest point of the Salt range. The main districts of the Salt range are Jhelum, Mianwali, and Chakwal.

| 4. Sutlej River | 4. Kurram River |
| 5. Beas River (tributary of Sutlej) | 5. Gomal River |

## Natural Topographical Features of the Indus Plain

The Indus plain is formed by the river Indus. It is divided into two plains: the Upper Indus Plain and the Lower Indus Plain.

**Active Flood Plain:** It is a narrow strip of land with rich alluvial deposits on either side of almost all the rivers, except for the southern half of the river Ravi. The active flood plain is almost regularly flooded every year. The active flood plain of the river Ravi is 40 km wide, which makes it important for agricultural activities such as crop cultivation and fish farming. Other features like meanders and ox-bow lakes are also present in the active flood plain, formed by the acts of erosion and deposition.

**Old Flood Plain:** An old floodplain is a high, flat area that was once flooded by a river. It is the area that lies between the active flood plain and the alluvial terraces. Old floodplains are valuable for agricultural activities and provide food, flood control, habitat, and recreation.

**Alluvial Terraces:** Alluvial terraces, or bars, are flat and higher grounds found at different elevations between the rivers or doabs. They are formed when the river changes its course over time and leaves behind its old floodplain. Alluvial terraces are typically made up of sand, silt, and clay, and are often very fertile and favor extensive farming. The prominent alluvial terraces are Nili Bar in Bari Doab, Sandal Bar in Rachna Doab, and Kirana Bar in Chaj Doab. Doabs attract population because of the availability of flat land for construction, settlements, and other industrial and business activities.

<SideActivity title="Do You Know?">
Doab refers to a tract of land lying between two rivers.
</SideActivity>

**Piedmont Plains or Alluvial Fans:** These plains are formed by the river as soon as it comes out of the mountains. These plains are agriculturally productive. Piedmont plains are located at the foothills of the Kirthar, Suleman, and Siwaliks of the Himalayan mountain ranges.

<SideActivity title="Do You Know?">
A piedmont plain is a low-lying area of land that lies at the base of a mountain range.
</SideActivity>

**Tidal Delta:** It is also called the Indus delta, located to the south of Thatta. It is a triangular or fan-shaped area with distributaries coming out from the main river. It plays a significant role in the ecology, economy, and culture of Pakistan.

**Cuestas:** Cuestas, or escarpments, are important features of the lower Indus Plain. These are limestone ridges gently sloping on one side and steep on the other side. Examples include the Rohri Cuesta and Gango Takkar in Hyderabad. These cuestas provide a firm foundation for the construction of barrages, which are used for the storage of water for irrigation and fishing purposes.

## Economic Activities in the Indus Plain

The Indus plain is a very productive region of Pakistan for a number of reasons. The fertile soil and plenty of water supply through a well-developed irrigation system make it ideal for agricultural activities. Availability of flat land and drainage makes it ideal for settlement growth. Industrialization is encouraged due to the presence of vast mineral deposits and a well-developed transport network of air, railways, and roads. Main urban centers are Lahore, Sialkot, Faisalabad, and Gujranwala.

# 4.6 Desert Areas


Pakistan has vast and hot deserts. They are located at three places.

a) The Thal desert is located between the river Indus and river Jhelum. It is also called the Sindh Sagar Doab.

b) To the southeast of Pakistan, the Thar desert is located in Sindh. It has three main desert areas: Cholistan, Nara, and Tharparkar.

c) The Kharan desert is a sandy and mountainous desert situated in Balochistan province in southwestern Pakistan.

Deserts have a very changing landscape due to strong winds. The most important relief features of desert areas are rolling sand dunes, undulating land, sand plains or strips of land, bare rock, dry soil due to lack of water, and cracks in rocks caused by intense heat and weathering action. Hot climate, lack of rainfall, and strong winds make desert areas a hard place to live because all the basic life-sustaining activities are hindered, such as farming. Natural vegetation in desert areas is a mixture of shrubs, succulents, herbs, and a few grasses. Availability of water to a major part of Thar was made possible through canals from the Sukkur Barrage, which has changed the area into farmland.

<CaptionedImage src="kg2dj00npwc6mnaegc79ekd2a98bvr2v" alt="Desert landscape in Pakistan" caption="Desert Areas of Pakistan" />

## Economic Potential of Desert Areas of Pakistan

Desert areas are rich in mineral deposits and fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and gas. These mineral deposits have the potential to play an important role in the development of Pakistan's economy. For example, the Cholistan desert is rich in lignite, gypsum, and silica sand. Moreover, the coal deposits in the Thar desert could be used to generate electricity, and the iron ore deposits could be used to produce steel.

## What I Have Learned

- Pakistan's natural topography is diverse, with mountains, deserts, plains, and coastlines.
- The northern highlands are home to some of the world's highest mountains, including the Karakoram Range, the Himalayas, and the Hindu Kush.
- The Indus plain is a vast agricultural region.
- The Indus River has been a source of water for irrigation and drinking for centuries, and it has also been a major transportation route.
- Pakistan has potential for renewable energy and tourism development.
- Desert areas are rich in mineral deposits such as fossil fuels.
