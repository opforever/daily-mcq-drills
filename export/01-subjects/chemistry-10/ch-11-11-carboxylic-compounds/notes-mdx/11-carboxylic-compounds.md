The organic compounds that contain carbon-oxygen double bonds $(\complement \mathrm{C}=0)$ are called carbonyl compounds. There are many types of carbonyl compounds, such as aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, etc. Carbonyl compounds in which carbonyl carbon is attached to the -OH (hydroxyl) group are called carboxylic acids. ## 1. By the Oxidation of Alcohols

Ethanol is readily oxidized to aldehyde, which further oxidizes to carboxylic acids in the presence of oxidizing agents such as potassium dichromate or potassium permanganate(VII) in an acidic medium. This process is fast but requires strong oxidizing agents.
<CaptionedImage src="kg2cgqhfg2npkzm6wzy3th582x8btfcs" alt="" caption="" />

The oxidizing agent provides nascent oxygen for the oxidation of alcohol. In the first step, oxygen an atom removes two hydrogen atoms from ethanol to form water and ethanal. In the second step, the oxygen atom oxidizes ethanal into ethanoic acid.

## 2. By the Bacterial Oxidation of Ethanol

Ethanol can be converted into vinegar by the action of micro-organisms called acetobacter in an aerobic environment. The reaction is catalysed by enzymes produced by acetobacter bacteria.

$$
\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{CH}_{2} \mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{l})}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}
$$

The fermentation is complete when the ethanol is fully converted to acetic acid. This can be confirmed by tasting or by measuring the acetic acid concentration using titration.
Typical vinegar contains about $4-8 \%$ acetic acid. This process is slow but environmentally friendly, uses renewable resources.

# 11.2 Reactions of Carboxylic Acids


The carboxyl group shows the chemistry of both the carbonyl (- and -) and the hydroxyl $(-\mathrm{OH})$ groups. In most reactions, the carboxyl group is retained. However, the reactivity of these molecules is due to the presence of the carbonyl group.
Carboxylic acids undergo the following types of reactions.
i) The reaction in which the hydrogen atom of the carboxyl group $(-\mathrm{C} \mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H})$ is involved (salt formation).
ii) The reaction in which the -OH group is replaced by another group $(-\mathrm{OH})$.
iii) The reactions involving the carboxyl group as a whole $(-\mathrm{COOH})$.

## 1. Reaction involving H -atom of the carboxyl group (Acidic properties of Carboxylic acid)

Carboxylic acids are weaker acids than mineral acids. They produce $\mathrm{H}^{+}$ion when dissolved in water
<CaptionedImage src="kg28qdkb0dt0jy0q1yxcwn05nn8bt1b7" alt="" caption="" />
(a) Reactions with bases

Carboxylic acids react with strong bases $(\mathrm{NaOH}, \mathrm{KOH})$ to form salts

$$
\underset{\text { Acetic acid }}{\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}}+\mathrm{NaOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})} \longrightarrow \underset{\text { Sodium Acetate }}{\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}_{(\mathrm{aq})}}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\eta)}
$$

(b) Reaction with carbonates and bicarbonates

Carboxylic acids decompose carbonate and bicarbonates evolving carbon dioxide gas with effervescence.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{Na}_{2} \mathrm{CO}_{3(\mathrm{aq})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{q})}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})} \\
& \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{NaHCO}_{3(\mathrm{aq})} \longrightarrow \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{q})}+\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}
\end{aligned}
$$

(c) Reactions with metals

Carboxylic acids react with active metals such as $\mathrm{Na}, \mathrm{K}, \mathrm{Ca}, \mathrm{Mg}$ etc to form their salts with the evolution of hydrogen gas,

$$
\underset{\text { Acetic acid }}{2 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}}+2 \mathrm{Na}_{(\mathrm{s})} \longrightarrow \underset{\text { Sodium Acetate }}{2 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}_{(\mathrm{aq})}}+\mathrm{H}_{2(q)}
$$

## Activity 11.1

Studying and analyzing the reactions between vinegar (acetic acid) and two different substances: sodium metal and sodium carbonate.
Materials Needed:

- Vinegar (acetic acid, $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}$ )
- Small pieces of sodium metal (Na) (Caution: Sodium metal is highly reactive and should be handled with extreme care under the supervision of a qualified instructor)
- Sodium carbonate $\left(\mathrm{Na}_{2} \mathrm{CO}_{3}\right)$
- Two small beakers or glass containers
- Droppers or pipettes
- Safety goggles and gloves

Safety Precautions

- Ensure all students wear safety goggles, gloves, and lab aprons.
- Explain the safety hazards associated with handling sodium metal and performing reactions that release gases.
- Conduct the experiment in a fume hood or a well-ventilated area.
- Sodium metal should be handled only with tweezers or tongs and kept away from water. Introduction to Chemical Reactions
- Briefly introduce the concept of chemical reactions, reactants, and products.
- Discuss the properties of vinegar (acetic acid), sodium metal, and sodium carbonate.
- Explain that vinegar is an acid that can react with metals and carbonates to produce different products.

## Procedure

- Place a small piece of sodium metal in a dry beaker.
- Using a dropper, carefully add a small amount of vinegar to the beaker with sodium metal.

## Observation

- Reaction: The sodium metal reacts vigorously with vinegar, producing bubbles and fizzing.
- Gas Released: Hydrogen gas $\left(\mathrm{H}_{2}\right)$ is released, and sodium acetate $\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}\right)$ is formed in the solution.

Discussion Points:

$$

- Why is hydrogen gas released?

## Reaction: Vinegar with Sodium Carbonate

Procedure:

- In a separate beaker, add a small amount (about a teaspoon) of sodium carbonate.
- Slowly add vinegar using a dropper until the bubbling reaction occurs.

Observation:

- Reaction: Vigorous bubbling and fizzing are observed as carbon dioxide gas $\left(\mathrm{CO}_{2}\right)$ is released.
- Gas Released: Carbon dioxide ( $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ ) is released, forming water ( $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ ) and sodium acetate $\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}\right)$.

## Chemical Equation:

$$
\mathrm{Na}_{2} \mathrm{CO}_{3(\mathrm{~s})}+2 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COONa}_{(\mathrm{aq})}+\mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}
$$

## Discussion Points:

- How can we identify the gas released?

# 11.3 Industrial Applications of Carboxylic Acids and Esters


Carboxylic acids and esters have many industrial applications.

1. Methanoic acid commonly known as formic acid is used in the textile industry as an acidreducing agent.
2. Ethanoic acid commonly known as acetic acid is used in making plastics and esters.
3. Sodium salts of fatty acids are used in making soaps and detergents.
4. Ethanoic acid is used in making medicines like aspirin.
5. Many carboxylic acids are used as industrial solvents.
6. Citric acid is a natural preservative and is used in both processed foods and drinks.
7. Many carboxylic acids are used as industrial solvents.
8. Carboxylic acids are used in the preparation of perfumes and artificial food essences.
9. Benzoic acid and sodium benzoate are used in the food industry, the pharmaceutical industry and the personal care industry. These are widely used for making jams, jellies, sauces, toothpaste, mouthwashes, lipsticks, and facial cleansers. Esters enhance the feel and performance of other ingredients in cosmetics. They hydrate, soften, and smooth the surface of skin texture so they are widely used in making lotions and creams. They also reduce the greasy feel of products.
10. Tartaric acid is used in baking powder. It reacts with carbonates in dough in the baking industry.
11. Esters are industrial solvents for dyes, glues, inks for permanent markers, and whiteboard markers.
12. Esters are used for making car spray paints, varnishes and nail polish remover.
13. Esters are used to making polymers called polyesters, which are used to make plastic bottles, canes, and textile clothing fibers.

# 11.4 Everyday Uses of Carboxylic Acids


- Ethanoic acid solution $(5 \%)$ is used as vinegar.
- Vinegar is used to make pickles, flavouring food, and dress salads.
- Lemon juice which contains ascorbic acid is used to reduce the smell of fish and flavouring food.
- Ascorbic acid is also used as vitamin-C.
- Benzoic acid and its salt sodium benzoate are used as preservatives in food and beverages to inhibit the growth of bacteria and fungi.

# 11.5 Everyday Uses of Esters


Esters are sweet-smelling organic compounds. They are used as;

- Food flavouring compounds as they give a characteristic odour to fruits.
- As fragrance and perfumes.
- Some esters are used in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis.
- Aspirin is an ester. It is used as a fever reducer and pain killer.
