# 14.1 Alkanes

The simplest organic compounds are hydrocarbons. Organic compounds that contain only two elements, carbon and hydrogen, are called hydrocarbons. There are many types of hydrocarbons. They are classified according to the type of bond between the carbon atoms. In the previous chapter, you learned the differences between alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes.

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons. They have general formula $\mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{c} 1}$. Each carbon atom forms four bonds and each hydrogen atom forms only one bond. # 14.2 General Methods of Preparations of Alkanes


## 1. By the hydrogenation of alkenes and alkynes

Addition of hydrogen molecule across carbon-carbon multiple-bond is called hydrogenation. Hydrogenation takes place in presence of finally divided nickel at 200-300°C and high pressure. Hydrogenation can also be done in presence of Pt or Pd at room temperature.

## 2. By the reduction of alkyl halides

When an alkyl halide is treated with Zn in presence of an aqueous acid, an alkane is produced. Usually aqueous solution of HCl or $\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}$ is used.

$$
\mathrm{CH}_{3}-\mathrm{Cl}+2[\mathrm{H}] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Zn} / \mathrm{HCl}_{(\mathrm{aq})}} \mathrm{CH}_{4}+\mathrm{HCl}
$$

Zn reacts with aqueous acid to liberate atomic hydrogen called nascent hydrogen. Nascent hydrogen reduces alkyl halide. Addition of nascent hydrogen is called reduction.

## 3. By the cracking of larger hydrocarbons

A large hydrocarbon (alkane) molecule breaks into smaller hydrocarbons when heated at high temperatures such as $450-750^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and high pressure. This process is called thermal cracking. This process produces a mixture of alkanes and alkenes. For example, when decane is heated at high temperature and high pressure, it breaks down into octane and ethene.

$$
\underset{\text { Decane }}{\mathrm{C}_{10} \mathrm{H}_{22}} \quad \rightarrow \quad \begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_{8} \mathrm{H}_{18}+\mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{4} \\
& \text { Octane Ethene }
\end{aligned}
$$

# 14.3 Properties of Alkanes


Alkane molecules are essentially non-polar. They are less dense than water and do not dissolve in it. Chemically, alkanes do not react with most ionic compounds. The lack of reactivity makes them useful solvents. For example, hexane is used to extract vegetable oils from corn, soybeans, cotton seeds, etc. Alkanes containing up to four carbon atoms are colourless and odourless gases. Alkanes, containing five to seventeen atoms, are colourless and odourless liquids. Higher alkanes are solids that are also colourless and odourless.

## 1. Halogenation

Although unreactive towards ionic substances, alkanes readily react with halogens in sunlight. A chemical reaction that takes place in presence of sun light is called as photochemical reaction.

The reaction of an alkane and a halogen is a substitution reaction. In this reaction a halogen atom substitutes for one or more of the hydrogen atoms of an alkane.
For examples the reaction of methane and chlorine in diffused sunlight occurs as follows.

$$
\mathrm{CH}_{4(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{Cl}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \xrightarrow{\text { diffused sunlight }} \underset{\text { Chloromethane }}{\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{Cl}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{HCl}_{(\mathrm{g})}}
$$

In direct sunlight the reaction of methane with chlorine is explosive and forms carbon and HCl .

$$
\mathrm{CH}_{4(\mathrm{~g})}+2 \mathrm{Cl}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \xrightarrow{\text { direct sunlight }} \mathrm{C}_{(\mathrm{s})}+4 \mathrm{HCl}_{(\mathrm{g})}
$$

## 2. Combustion

A reaction of a substance with oxygen or air that causes the rapid release of heat and the appearance of a flame is called combustion. Complete combustion of an alkane produces carbon dioxide, water and heat. Most of alkanes burn with blue flame.
For example, following reaction occurs when natural gas is burned.

$$
\mathrm{CH}_{4(\mathrm{~g})}+2 \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow \mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+2 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\text { heat }
$$

The lighter alkanes are widely used as fuel. This is because:

(i) Their combustion can be controlled.
(ii) They produce large amount of heat per gram.
(iii) They are cheap and readily available.

Incomplete combustion occurs in presence of limited supply of oxygen. Incomplete combustion of methane gives $\mathrm{CO}, \mathrm{C}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$.

$$
3 \mathrm{CH}_{4(\mathrm{~g})}+4 \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{CO}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{C}_{(\mathrm{s})}+6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{g})}
$$

## KEY POINTS

- Hydrocarbons are compounds that contain carbon and hydrogen only.
- The simplest hydrocarbon that is possible is $\mathrm{CH}_{4}$.
- Alkanes are generally unreactive
- Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons
- In a substation reaction one atom or a group of atoms is replaced by another atom or a group of atoms.
- Addition of hydrogen molecule in an unsaturated hydrocarbon is called hydrogenation.
