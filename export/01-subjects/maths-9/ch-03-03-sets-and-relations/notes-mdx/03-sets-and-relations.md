Definition:
A set is a well-defined collection of distinct objects.
The term well-defined, means that the objects follow a given discipline with which presence or absence of some object in the set is checked.

A set may consist of objects of different types.

Set Builder Form (Rule Method)

e.g. $\mathrm{A}=$ Set of all integers, can be written in set builder form as
$$
A=\{x \mid x \in Z\} \text { and read as }
$$
"A is the set of all elements $x$ such that $x$ belongs to Z"

Set Operations
Union of Sets
Let A and B be two given sets. Then union of A and B is the set of all those elements which are taken either from A or from B or from both.

The union of $A$ and $B$ is denoted by ' $A \cup B$ ' and:

$$
\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{~B}=\{x \mid x \in \mathrm{~A} \vee x \in \mathrm{~B}\}
$$

e.g. If $\mathrm{A}=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and $\mathrm{B}=\{3,4,1,7,6\}$, then:
$$
A \cup B=\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}
$$

Intersection of Sets
Let A and B be given sets then the intersection of A and B is the set of elements which belong to both A and B.
The intersection of A and B is denoted by ' $\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B}$ ' and:

$$
\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{~B}=\{x \mid x \in \mathrm{~A} \wedge x \in \mathrm{~B}\}
$$

e.g. If $\mathrm{A}=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and $\mathrm{B}=\{3,4,1,7,6\}$, then:

$$
A \cap B=\{1,3,4\}
$$

Difference of Sets
Difference of two sets A and B, denoted by A - B is a collection of those elements of A which are not present in B . For the two sets A and B :

$$
\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{B}=\{x \mid x \in \mathrm{~A} \wedge x \notin \mathrm{~B}\}
$$

Let $\mathrm{A}=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and $\mathrm{B}=\{3,4,6,7,8\}$, then:

$$
A-B=\{1,2,3,4,5\}-\{3,4,6,7,8\}=\{1,2,5\}
$$

In general, $\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{B} \neq \mathrm{B}-\mathrm{A}$
Complement of a Set
Let $\mathrm{A} \subset \mathrm{U}$ (i.e. A is proper subset of the universal set). Then the set of all elements of U , which are not in A , is called complement of A .
Complement of A is denoted by $\mathrm{A}^{c}$ or $\mathrm{A}^{\prime}=\mathrm{U}-\mathrm{A}$ and is defined as:

$$
\{x \mid x \in U \wedge x \notin \mathrm{~A}\}
$$

e.g. If $\mathrm{U}=\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}, \mathrm{A}=\{2,4,6\}$, then:

$$
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{c}}=U-\mathrm{A}=\{1,3,5,7\}
$$

# 3.2 Verification of Associative Laws Using Venn Diagram

## Veriffeation of Associative Laws Using Venn Diagram

We illustrate the concept with the help of following examples.
Associative Property of Union

$$
(A \cup B) \cup C=A \cup(B \cup C)
$$

Proof:

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text { Let } & x \in(\mathrm{~A} \cup \mathrm{~B}) \cup \mathrm{C} \\
\Rightarrow & x \in(\mathrm{~A} \cup \mathrm{~B}) \text { or } x \in \mathrm{C} \\
\Rightarrow & (x \in \mathrm{~A} \text { or } x \in \mathrm{~B}) \text { or } x \in \mathrm{C} \\
\Rightarrow & x \in \mathrm{~A} \text { or }(x \in \mathrm{~B} \text { or } x \in \mathrm{C}) \\
\Rightarrow & x \in \mathrm{~A} \text { or } x \in(\mathrm{~B} \cup \mathrm{C}) \\
\Rightarrow & x \in \mathrm{~A} \cup(\mathrm{~B} \cup \mathrm{C}) \\
\Rightarrow & (\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{~B}) \cup \mathrm{C} \subseteq \mathrm{~A} \cup(\mathrm{~B} \cup \mathrm{C}) \tag{a}
\end{array}
$$

Similarly, we can prove that:

$$
\begin{equation*}
A \cup B \cup C) \subseteq(A \cup B) \cup C \tag{b}
\end{equation*}
$$

From (a) and (b), we have:

$$
(A \cup B) \cup C=A \cup(B \cup C)
$$

Solution: Number of elements in $\mathrm{P}=n(\mathrm{P})=2$ Number of elements in $\mathrm{Q}=n(\mathrm{Q})=2$ Number of elements in $\mathrm{P} \times \mathrm{Q}=n(\mathrm{P} \times \mathrm{Q})=n(\mathrm{P}) \times n(\mathrm{Q})$

Number of binary relations in $\mathrm{P} \times \mathrm{Q}=2^{\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{P} \times \mathrm{Q})}$
