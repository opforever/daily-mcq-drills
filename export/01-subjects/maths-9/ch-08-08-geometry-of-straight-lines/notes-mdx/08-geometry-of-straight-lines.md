# Inclination of Straight Line

## Measuring Line Inclination

The inclination of a straight line is the angle $\theta$ which the line makes with positive x-axis measured in anti-clockwise direction.

# Gradient of Straight Line

## Horizontal and Vertical Lines

- Any line parallel to x-axis is called horizontal line.
- Any line parallel to $y$-axis is called vertical line.
- Line which is neither horizontal nor vertical is called oblique line.
- Inclination of horizontal line is $0^{\circ}$.

## Gradient

When a car rides up a hill, its speed reduces. The steeper the hill, the harder it is to climb.
The measure of the steepness of hill is called gradient (slope).
Gradient is ratio of the vertical distance to the horizontal distance.

## Gradient and Inclination Relationship

The gradient of a straight line is the tangent of the angle which the line makes with the positive direction of the x-axis.

## Gradient of Straight Line

The gradient of straight line is defined as:

$$
\tan \theta=\frac{\text { rise }}{\text { run }}
$$

In the figure line AB is inclined at an angle $\theta$ with x-axis.

$$
\text { Gradient of line } \begin{aligned}
A B & =\frac{\text { rise }}{\text { run }} \\
& =\frac{\text { difference in } y-\text { coordinates of } A B}{\text { difference in } x-\text { coordinates of } A B} \\
& =\frac{9-2}{8-4}=\frac{7}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<CaptionedImage src="kg2fxb6ma5nqgzm3hraqx0qdkn8bv381" alt="" caption="" />
Alternatively, gradient of $\mathrm{AB}=\frac{2-9}{4-8}=\frac{-7}{-4}=\frac{7}{4}$

Generally, gradient of a straight line is denoted by $m$.
Therefore, $m=\tan \theta$
Example 1:

(i) Find the gradient (slope) of line whose inclination is $45^{\circ}$.
(ii) Find inclination of line whose gradient is 1.732.

Solution:

(i) Here $\theta=45^{\circ}$
$$
\text { ∴ Gradient }=\tan 45^{\circ}=1
$$
(ii) Gradient $=\tan \theta=1.732$
$$
\text { ∴ Inclination }=\theta=\tan ^{-1}(1.732)=60^{\circ}
$$

# Formula of Gradient

## Calculating the Gradient

If $\mathrm{A}\left(x_{1}, \mathrm{y}_{1}\right)$ and $\mathrm{B}\left(x_{2}, \mathrm{y}_{2}\right)$ are two points on a line AB, Then gradient of $\overrightarrow{A B}$ is: ${ }^{-7}$

$$
m=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}=\frac{y_{1}-y_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}
$$

Gradient of AB can not be $\frac{y_{1}-y_{2}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}$ or $\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}$
<CaptionedImage src="kg24yz290gb7m7v6kettxd5g198bthjj" alt="" caption="" />

Key Fact

- If the line is parallel to x -axis, then $\theta=0^{\circ}$, and we have $m=\tan 0^{\circ}=0$. Thus, gradient of horizontal line is 0 .
- If the line is perpendicular to $x$-axis, then $\theta=90^{\circ}$, and we have $m=\tan 90^{\circ}$ which is undefined. Thus, gradient of vertical line is undefined.

Figure 1 shows two parallel lines $l_{1}$ and $l_{2}$ making angle $\alpha$ with positive x-axis.
If gradients of $l_{1}$ and $l_{2}$ are $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ respectively then:

$$
m_{1}=m_{2}
$$

Figure 2 shows two perpendicular lines $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ making angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with positive x-axis respectively. If gradients of $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ are $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ respectively then:

$$
m_{1}=-\frac{1}{m_{2}} \text { or } m_{2}=-\frac{1}{m_{1}}
$$

Key Fact

- If two non-vertical lines are parallel, there gradients are equal.
- If two lines have same gradients, they are parallel.
- If $m_{1}$ and $m_{2}$ are gradients of two perpendicular lines, then:
$$
m_{1} \times m_{2}=-1
$$
- Three points $\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}$ and C are collinear if gradients of $\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{BC}$ and AC are equal.

Example 2:
Find gradients of lines joining: (i) $\mathrm{A}(1,4), \mathrm{B}(3,6)$ (ii) $\mathrm{C}(-3,5), \mathrm{D}(0,3)$
Solution:

(i) Gradient of $\mathrm{AB}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}=\frac{6-4}{3-1}=1$
(ii) Gradient of $\mathrm{CD}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}=\frac{3-5}{0-(-3)}=\frac{-2}{3}$

Example 3:
Prove that lines AB and CD are parallel where $\mathrm{A}(3,5), \mathrm{B}(4,6), \mathrm{C}(7,11), \mathrm{D}(9,13)$.
Solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Gradient of } \mathrm{AB}=m_{1}=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}=\frac{6-5}{4-3}=1 \\
& \text { Gradient of } \mathrm{CD}=m_{2}=\frac{13-11}{9-7}=\frac{2}{2}=1 \\
& \text { As, } m_{1}=m_{2} \\
& \text { Therefore, } \mathrm{AB} \text { and } \mathrm{CD} \text { are parallel lines. }
\end{aligned}
$$

Example 4:
Lines AB and CD with coordinates $\mathrm{A}(0,3), \mathrm{B}(2,8), \mathrm{C}(3,10), \mathrm{D}(8, \mathrm{k})$ are perpendicular. Find k .
Solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Gradient of } \mathrm{AB}=m_{1}=\frac{8-3}{2-0}=\frac{5}{2} \\
& \text { Gradient of } \mathrm{CD}=m_{2}=\frac{k-10}{8-3}=\frac{k-10}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

As, AB and CD are perpendicular, Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{lc}
\Rightarrow & \frac{5}{2} \times \frac{k-10}{5}=-1 \\
\Rightarrow & \frac{5 k-50}{10}=-1
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\Rightarrow & 5 k-50=-10 & \Rightarrow \\
\Rightarrow & 5 k=40 & \Rightarrow \\
\Rightarrow & k=8
\end{array}
$$
